**Series:** The Bell Beneath Falls Park
**Format:** 7-Episode Audio Drama Series
**Genre:** Dark Mystery / Southern Gothic
**Author:** Iris Fontaine
**Status:** DRAFT — for Marc's review

## SERIES OVERVIEW

**Logline:**
A woman investigates her mother's twenty-nine-year-old disappearance through six hidden brass tokens — Camperdown Mill flood tokens, each stamped with an inverted clapper that marks not a warning but a burial — mapping a forgotten underground route beneath Falls Park in Greenville, South Carolina, concealed for decades by forged city property records.

**Premise:**
MARA VANCE grew up believing her mother CLAIRE chose to leave when Mara was seven years old. That belief shaped everything — her work, her relationships, the particular shape of her silence when someone asked about family. Twenty-nine years later, a dying man named ADRIAN CROSS slipped a brass token into her hand in a tunnel beneath Liberty Bridge and said two words: *the bell knows.* 

What Mara finds in the tunnels beneath Greenville challenges everything she understood about her mother, her city, and the five people whose photographs Claire pinned to a wall in a hidden room before she disappeared.

**Tone:**
Southern Gothic mystery with procedural grounding. The atmosphere of something long-buried pressing upward — old infrastructure, old grief, old debt. The rational (property records, police procedure, historical archives) and the uncanny (burial marks, a dead woman's recorded voice, a route that leads somewhere no one was supposed to know existed) exist side by side and neither fully explains the other. Iris Fontaine territory: the answer is always logical and never quite enough.

**Setting:**
Greenville, South Carolina — present day. The Reedy River corridor, Liberty Bridge and the maintenance tunnels beneath it, the Falls Park waterfall and its sub-basement infrastructure, the ruins of the old Camperdown Mill site, the Greenville City Planning office, the university archives at Furman. The city is beautiful on the surface; the series lives in what is underneath.

## SERIES CENTRAL QUESTION

**Who murdered Claire Vance — and what did she find in the underground route beneath Falls Park that someone was willing to kill to protect?**

## CHARACTER GUIDE

### MARA VANCE — Protagonist / Narrator POV
**Age:** 35–38 (exact age calibrated to timeline ruling — see TIMELINE NOTE)
**Background:** Works in investigative archival research — not a detective, but someone who knows how to find things in public records, an instinct honed partly by the unanswered question at the center of her childhood. She has never stopped looking for Claire. She has done it quietly, the way people carry grief they believe is partly their own fault.
**Enters the Series:** Believing her mother chose to leave. This belief is the wound.
**Arc:** 
- Ep1–2: The wound reopens. Claire was not absent — she was fighting. Mara's grief converts to purpose.
- Ep3–4: Mara discovers she has been maneuvered. Someone knew she would eventually come looking and built a path. The question becomes: were the tokens left *for* her, or left to use *her*?
- Ep5: Betrayal. A trust is broken. Mara is isolated, weaponless, and for the first time genuinely afraid.
- Ep6–7: Mara stops following the trail others laid and starts cutting her own. She completes what Claire tried to do — not as a daughter collecting her mother's work, but as someone doing the work herself.
**Final State:** Not healed, but clear. She knows what happened. She made sure it reached the right people. She did not do it the way Claire planned, but she thinks Claire would understand.
**Voice:** Third person limited. Narrated by June Harlow (American Southern Gothic, velvety, draws you close).

---

### CLAIRE VANCE — Mara's Mother (Absent / Present Through Recordings and Evidence)
**Age at disappearance:** Mid-30s
**When she disappeared:** June 18, 1997
**Who she was:** An archivist and amateur historian with a gift for noticing discrepancies in public records. She worked for a Greenville civic organization — close enough to city planning to see things that didn't add up. She was also a mother. The series must never let the audience forget that second thing.
**What she found:** While researching the history of Camperdown Mill for a centennial project, Claire discovered that the underground flood-infrastructure tunnels beneath the mill site — which official city records described as decommissioned and sealed — were not only intact but extended beneath what became Falls Park. The decommissioning records had been forged. Whoever forged them was protecting something at the end of the tunnel.
**What she did about it:** She didn't go to the police immediately — LENA ORTIZ's involvement makes this complicated in hindsight. She built a suspect board. She hid the first Camperdown night token in the Liberty Bridge tunnel and left instructions for the others. She recorded a cassette. She prepared, the way careful people prepare.
**Why she was killed:** She was killed before she could complete her investigation. The forged records concealed fraud on a scale large enough to destroy careers and a development empire. Claire was too close and had begun to trust the wrong person.
**How she appears:** Only through recordings (the cassette in Ep1, a second recording in Ep6), photographs, handwritten notes in the hidden room, and the system of tokens she prepared.

---

### DETECTIVE LENA ORTIZ — Greenville Police Department
**Age:** Mid-40s
**First appearance:** Already in the Liberty Bridge tunnel when Mara arrives — Ep1.
**Surface read:** Suspicious. She knew about the tunnel. She didn't announce she was investigating Claire's disappearance.
**Actual role:** Lena has been building a case against SILAS CROWE for two years, in near-total secrecy, because the first time she tried to move on it, the case file disappeared and she received an informal warning. She is protecting the investigation by controlling who touches it — including Mara, whose arrival risks exposing what Lena has built before it's ready. This makes Lena look like an antagonist in Ep1–4.
**The complication:** Lena knew Claire. They were, briefly, collaborators. Claire's death is the reason Lena is still on this case and why she cannot let it go. She carries the guilt of someone who gave the wrong assurance at the wrong moment.
**Arc:** Lena begins as an apparent threat. She becomes an uneasy ally. In Ep5, she does the thing that looks like betrayal and is actually protection. By the finale, she and Mara make the difference together — but they don't become friends. The trust is functional, not warm.
**Clue to her character:** She was already in the tunnel. Not because she was chasing Mara — because she had been watching the tunnel for weeks, waiting for the person Crowe's associate would eventually send to interfere.

---

### ELI MERCER — Historian, Furman University, Greenville
**Age:** Mid-40s
**Specialty:** Greenville industrial history, Camperdown Mill, Reedy River infrastructure
**First appearance:** Ep1 — follows Mara into the tunnel after she explicitly told him not to.
**Why he's suspicious:** He contacted Mara out of nowhere eight weeks before Ep1, with Claire's name and records Mara had never seen. He knew about the flood tokens before she asked. He is on Claire's suspect board. And there is a blood-dark key taped behind his photograph in the hidden room.
**Actual role:** Eli is innocent of Claire's murder but not innocent. He was Adrian Cross's research partner and knew the general shape of what Claire had found. When Adrian decided to contact Mara, Eli agreed and then panicked — he contacted Mara *first*, before Adrian, in an attempt to control what she found and in what order. He believed he was protecting her. He was also protecting himself, because his name appears on a grant application signed by the same city official who forged the property records — he didn't know that connection existed until Ep4.
**The key:** Claire put the key behind Eli's photo as a message, not an accusation. The key was Eli's — something he gave Claire as a research tool, connected to a city archive he had access to. Claire's choice of where to put it was her way of saying: *start with him, but start gently.*
**Arc:** From suspect to reluctant ally. Eli's usefulness is his knowledge — he understands the underground infrastructure better than anyone. His liability is that he knows more than he's admitted, and Mara will not forgive him for that easily.
**Claire's warning:** "Do not trust the person who brings you to the bell." This refers to Eli. Claire meant: trust him conditionally, verify everything, and do not let him decide what you find first. She was right that he would try to manage the investigation. She was wrong that this made him an enemy.

---

### ADRIAN CROSS — Journalist / Investigator (Deceased, Ep1)
**Age at death:** Early 60s
**Background:** Spent decades as an investigative journalist before retiring to independent research. Took up Claire's case approximately four years before Ep1, through contacts in the historical archive community.
**Role in Ep1:** He is dying when Mara finds him — poisoned, slowly, by something that was in a drink he accepted from someone he trusted. He knows he is dying. He arranged to meet Mara in the tunnel because the tunnel is the one place he was certain he wouldn't be interfered with before he could pass on what he had.
**What he passes on:** The first Camperdown token, and the dying words that orient the whole series: *the bell knows.*
**Significance beyond Ep1:** Adrian's research files are scattered — partially with Lena (who took copies), partially destroyed, partially in a location only Eli knows. Recovering his work is one of the through-lines of Ep2–3.

---

### SILAS CROWE — Developer (Antagonist)
**Age:** 55–60
**Role:** Principal of a Greenville-area development company with significant commercial real estate interests adjacent to Falls Park. His permits and land rights are built on the fraudulent decommissioning records for the underground tunnels.
**Motive:** The underground route, if officially acknowledged, would require city re-evaluation of the land above it. Crowe's permit for a major mixed-use development sits directly over the tunnel corridor. Acknowledgment = years of delay at minimum, potential invalidation of his land rights, investigation of how those rights were obtained.
**What he did:** He did not personally forge the records. He directed a city official — the Greenville Planning Department deputy director — to do so, in exchange for significant private consideration. He did not personally arrange Claire's death. He arranged for it to be arranged — one layer removed, as he always operates.
**Texture:** Expensive jacket. Practiced blankness. The particular quality of someone who has learned that expressions cost money. He is not a monster in the way that obvious villains are monsters; he is the kind of person who would, if confronted, say with genuine conviction that he had no choice, that the city needed the development, that Claire would have caused more harm than she prevented. He would mean it. This is more frightening than the alternative.
**First physical appearance:** Ep4, when Mara traces the property records. He appears briefly and dangerously — the scene where he shows up at a place Mara is researching before she announced she'd be there.

---

### JUNE BELL — Retired Camperdown Mill Worker
**Age at death:** Late 70s
**Background:** One of the last surviving workers from Camperdown Mill's final years of operation. Knew the flood-watch system firsthand, remembered carrying the night tokens, and remembered which workers carried the ones with the inverted clapper.
**What she knows:** June knows more than she admits in Ep3. She knows the burial marks were used — she witnessed it, once, as a young woman, and was told by the shift foreman to forget she'd seen it. She forgot for decades. Then someone came around asking questions, maybe fifteen years ago, and she remembered again.
**Why she's killed:** She talked to Mara in Ep3. Whoever monitors the tunnel network — Crowe's operative — has a list of people who have been contacted. June Bell was on it. Her death is the series' first confirmation that the stakes are real and the antagonist is watching in near-real-time.
**Significance:** Her death is also the series' proof that this is not a cold case in the comfortable sense. It is live. Someone is still protecting it.

## THE TOKEN MECHANIC

**Origin:** Camperdown Cotton Mill operated on the Reedy River in Greenville from the late nineteenth century through the mid-twentieth. During flood-watch periods, workers walked a checkpoint route along the river and through the mill's underground infrastructure, verifying that the flood gates, overflow channels, and drainage points were functional. They carried brass tokens — about the size of a silver dollar, heavier — stamped with the mill's bell symbol on one face and a striker symbol on the other.

**Normal use:** A worker who completed a checkpoint successfully stamped their token against a pressure plate at each station. The upright striker on the stamp meant: flood threat confirmed or clear, checkpoint logged, no unusual findings.

**The inverted clapper:** When a worker found a body at a checkpoint — or was directed to mark a body that had been placed in the flood infrastructure — they used the inverted-clapper stamp. It created a different impression in the ledger. In the mill's accounting, an inverted clapper simply meant "no flood activity at this station." In practice, the workers who carried them knew it meant: something is buried here.

**Six tokens, six checkpoints:** The six tokens Claire located and hid map the underground route from the Liberty Bridge maintenance entrance through the Reedy River flood infrastructure, through the sealed tunnels beneath the old Camperdown Mill site, to a sub-basement chamber beneath Falls Park waterfall. Each token was originally placed at a waypoint checkpoint by Claire — each one marks a turn in the route. Together they are a map you have to walk to read.

**Physical appearance:** Brass, worn smooth at the edges from decades of handling. The bell impression on one face is clear. The striker impression on the other is either upright (token from normal flood-watch use) or inverted (burial token). EP1's token is the first — an original mill token, not one Claire placed. It was Adrian's. He got it from Claire before she disappeared.

## THE UNDERGROUND ROUTE

**What it is:** A network of flood-management tunnels and infrastructure chambers built beneath the Reedy River corridor in Greenville during the Camperdown Mill era. The main passage runs approximately half a mile from the Liberty Bridge maintenance entrance to a sealed chamber beneath the Falls Park waterfall. Several branch passages connect to structures that no longer exist above ground.

**Current official status:** Decommissioned. City records show the tunnels were inspected, sealed with concrete fills, and removed from the active infrastructure map 1997 — around the time of Claire's disappearance. The decommissioning reports are signed, stamped, and filed correctly in the city planning archive. They are also entirely fabricated.

**Actual status:** Structurally sound throughout most of the route. The concrete fills shown in the decommissioning reports do not exist. The tunnels are dark, damp, and forgotten — but navigable.

**What the route leads to:** A sealed chamber beneath the waterfall in Falls Park — the original mill's deep inspection vault, designed to allow engineers to access the foundation of the water-management system. The chamber contains: the evidence Claire assembled before she disappeared; original Camperdown Mill land records proving the underground infrastructure was legally public property at the time the fraudulent decommissioning was filed; and confirmation that the land above, now Crowe's development site, cannot be legally privatized without city re-authorization that was never sought.

**The six waypoints:**
- Checkpoint 1: Liberty Bridge maintenance tunnel (beneath Liberty Bridge) — token held by Adrian Cross, passed to Mara in Ep1
- Checkpoint 2: River overflow chamber (beneath the Reedy River bank, near the old millrace) — Ep2
- Checkpoint 3: Camperdown Mill north foundation passage (beneath the mill ruins) — Ep3
- Checkpoint 4: The junction chamber (where three tunnels meet — the intersection that explains why the forged records needed to be filed in three separate departments) — Ep4
- Checkpoint 5: Falls Park maintenance sub-basement (beneath the park's modern utility infrastructure) — Ep5
- Checkpoint 6: The sealed chamber beneath the waterfall — Ep6

## THE FORGED PROPERTY RECORDS

**What was forged:** Three sets of city planning records, filed across three separate departments (Infrastructure, Environmental, Property Management) between 1997 and 1999, showing that the Camperdown Mill underground infrastructure had been decommissioned, concrete-filled, and removed from the active city asset register.

**Who forged them:** The deputy director of the Greenville City Planning Department — a man who has since been promoted and now sits on a committee that reviews development permits. He acted at Silas Crowe's direction, for private compensation.

**What the forgery concealed:** The existence and accessibility of the underground route; the fact that the land above portions of the route had not been properly reclassified before being permitted for private development; and the original mill land records, which remained on file in the sealed chamber and would have complicated Crowe's entire land acquisition if discovered.

**Claire's angle:** She found the discrepancy not in the forged records themselves — which were well-made — but in the pre-forgery maintenance requests. Routine city maintenance requests from the years just before the decommissioning referenced infrastructure that the decommissioning records claimed didn't exist yet. The wrong records were forged; the right ones were left in place.

**Eli's connection:** His historical research grant, applied for three years before Ep1, was approved with a signature from the same deputy director who forged the property records. This is likely coincidence — the deputy director approved many grants. But the signature appears in Eli's records and will appear in the investigation. He didn't know the connection. Finding it will not feel like coincidence to Mara.

## ARC STRUCTURE — 7 EPISODES

**Series Central Question:** Who murdered Claire Vance, and what does the underground route beneath Falls Park lead to?

**Series Finale Resolution Type:** Justice + Revelation (the conspiracy is publicly exposed; Crowe and the deputy director are confronted with evidence; Claire's assembled work completes its intended purpose)

**Protagonist Arc:** Mara begins as a woman carrying a grief built on a false premise — she thought her mother left her. She ends as someone who finished what her mother started. The difference between those two versions of Mara is the series.

### EPISODE SUMMARIES

**Ep1 — "The Flood Token"**
Mara enters the Liberty Bridge maintenance tunnel to meet Adrian Cross, a dying man with proof her mother was murdered. She finds Adrian dying, a Detective already in the tunnel, and Eli — who she told to stay back — following her in. Adrian presses the first Camperdown token into her hand. Eli reveals what the inverted clapper means. A hidden room reveals five photographs, Mara's own photograph, a blood-dark key taped behind Eli's photo, and a cassette recorder. Claire's voice: *do not trust the person who brings you to the bell.* A figure runs through the tunnel. Lena pursues. A gunshot.
*Cliffhanger type:* DANGER + REVELATION implied

**Ep2 — "The Burial Mark"**
Mara surfaces from the tunnel into a Greenville that has officially ruled Adrian Cross's death an accident. Lena survived — but won't say who she was chasing, or what she found. Mara follows the first token's route to checkpoint 2 (beneath the Reedy riverbank) and finds the second token. Eli makes a partial admission: he knew Adrian had contacted her before Eli did, and he reached out first to — in his word — prepare her. The second cassette message (left by Claire at checkpoint 2) establishes the route exists and names what it leads to.
*Cliffhanger type:* BETRAYAL — Mara verifies that Eli knew about the tunnel and Adrian's death before she called him to come.

**Ep3 — "The Mill Bell"**
Mara seeks out June Bell, the retired Camperdown Mill worker on Claire's board, to understand the burial-mark tradition. June confirms the history and admits she once saw the inverted clapper used — but claims she doesn't know anything about murders or tunnels or missing women. She is lying, and Mara suspects it. The third token is found in the mill ruins, with evidence that Claire had been there. Hours after Mara's visit, June Bell is found dead.
*Cliffhanger type:* DISCOVERY — Someone knew Mara visited June and acted within hours. The antagonist has visibility into Mara's movements.

**Ep4 — "The Forged Record"**
Mara traces the property records discrepancy Claire originally identified. The paper trail leads to the Greenville Planning Department and a deputy director whose name appears on the fraudulent decommissioning filings. Then she finds the same signature on Eli Mercer's grant application. The fourth token is found in a city archive box that Eli's access key can open — the same key that was taped behind his photograph. Mara and Eli use it together, an uneasy partnership. Silas Crowe appears at the archive building before Mara announced she'd be there.
*Cliffhanger type:* REVELATION — the deputy director's signature connects the forgery to someone still in power. And Crowe knew where Mara was going.

**Ep5 — "The Fifth Token"**
Mara is close to the vault. The fifth token is in the Falls Park maintenance sub-basement — she can feel the end of the route. She is also being watched. Lena warns her to wait — the case isn't ready. Mara doesn't wait. She enters the sub-basement and finds the fifth token. On her way out, she is stopped — not by Crowe's people, but by Lena, who physically prevents her from leaving and explains what she has been building and why Mara walking out with the fifth token right now would destroy two years of work.
*Cliffhanger type:* DECISION — Mara has the fifth token and knows where the sixth is. Lena is asking her to stop. Mara has to decide: trust the woman who looks like she's working against her, or go alone to the vault.

**Ep6 — "The Hidden Room"**
Mara and Lena reach the vault together — a compromise neither is comfortable with. The sealed chamber beneath Falls Park waterfall contains everything Claire assembled: documents, photographs, original mill land records, and a second cassette. Claire's second recording is longer — a full account of what she found, who she told, and what she feared. Then Crowe's man arrives. Fire in the sub-basement passage — the route behind them goes. Mara and Lena escape. The chamber burns. Mara has one thing: the sixth token, which was physically a different kind of token — not brass but iron, sealed, and with a small cavity. Inside the cavity: a CompactFlash card, waterproofed. Claire's digital backup.
*Cliffhanger type:* REVERSAL — the physical evidence is gone but the evidence isn't. Claire had a backup. She prepared for this too.

**Ep7 — "The Bell Speaks" (FINALE)**
Using the CompactFlash card, Mara and Lena reconstruct Claire's case with the digital records intact. The deputy director, confronted with what Claire found and what Mara has assembled, breaks first — he knows Crowe will not protect him and he has been waiting for someone to offer him a way out. Crowe is confronted publicly. The fraudulent property records are voided. The development permits are frozen pending investigation. Claire's disappearance is officially reclassified as homicide. The series ends not with Mara standing over the ruins of an enemy but with a simpler image: Mara pressing play on Claire's second cassette, alone this time, in a quiet room, hearing her mother's voice without urgency for the first time.
*Series finale: no cliffhanger.*

## CLIFFHANGER CHAIN (required before Ep1 production)

**Ep1:** Lena went after a figure in the tunnel. A gun fired once, deep in the stone, and then nothing. Eli holds the key Claire taped behind his photograph. And Claire's voice is still saying: *do not trust the person who brings you to the bell.*
→ Specific question: **Did Eli know the tunnel before Mara called him — and if so, why did he follow her in?**

**Ep2:** Eli confirms he knew. He contacted Mara before Adrian did, deliberately, to control what she found first. This is not the whole truth and Mara knows it isn't.
→ Specific question: **What else did Eli know — and how long has he known it?**

**Ep3:** June Bell is dead within hours of talking to Mara. Someone is watching Mara's contacts in near-real-time.
→ Specific question: **Who is monitoring Mara's movements — and how?**

**Ep4:** The forgery signature links to a still-active city official. Silas Crowe appeared at the archive before Mara told anyone she was going there.
→ Specific question: **How does Crowe know where Mara is — and is the city official already aware Mara is closing in?**

**Ep5:** Lena stops Mara from leaving the sub-basement with the fifth token. She is asking Mara to trust her. Two years of secret work are at stake.
→ Specific question: **Can Mara trust Lena — and what happens to the investigation if she can't?**

**Ep6:** The chamber burns. The physical evidence is gone. The sixth token is iron, sealed, containing a CompactFlash card. Claire made a backup. Claire remains sole author of all six tokens — Adrian did not open or update any token.
→ Specific question: **Is what Claire left on the drive enough — and does Crowe know it exists?**

## MARA'S EMOTIONAL ARC (detailed)

**The Wound (entering the series):**
Mara was seven when Claire stopped coming home. She was told — gently, then less gently, by the adults around her — that Claire had her reasons. *She left because something in her needed to go.* Mara grew up interpreting her mother's absence as abandonment, which meant she grew up interpreting herself as the kind of person who gets left. This is the wound the series runs through.

**The Rupture (Ep1):**
Claire's cassette voice does not sound like a woman who left. It sounds like a woman who prepared, who thought of her daughter, who tried to leave a trail. *I did not choose to go.* Everything Mara believed about her childhood reorganizes around those seven words.

**The Conversion (Ep2–3):**
Grief converts to purpose. Mara is good at finding things in records — this is not an accident, it is the thing she spent her adult life building so she would be ready for this. She's capable and she knows it. The series doesn't let her win easily, but it respects her competence.

**The Complication (Ep3–4):**
She begins to wonder if the path was too convenient. The tokens, the cassette, the board in the hidden room — Claire built a system for someone to find. Was it always meant for Mara? Or was it meant for someone else, and Mara was steered here? The question of agency — whether she is investigating or being used — introduces paranoia into the series.

**The Breaking Point (Ep5):**
Lena stopping her feels like proof that nobody is trustworthy. The breaking point is not that Mara is betrayed — it's that she cannot tell the difference between betrayal and protection, and that uncertainty is its own undoing.

**The Resolution (Ep6–7):**
Mara stops asking whether she can trust people and starts making deliberate choices about whom to trust and for what. This is different from naive trust. It's the competence the wound had been hiding all along — she was always capable of this judgment. She just hadn't needed to exercise it before. The finale is Mara not being her mother's daughter completing her mother's mission. It's Mara being herself, completing something she chose to take on.

**The Final Image:**
Mara plays Claire's second cassette alone, in a quiet room, with no urgency. She presses play and listens to her mother's voice without needing it to tell her anything she doesn't already know. She listens because she wants to, not because she has to. That's the ending.

## VARIETY GUARDRAILS — SERIES-LEVEL

Across the seven episodes, the following must be varied:

- **Cliffhanger types:** Ep1=Danger/Revelation, Ep2=Betrayal, Ep3=Discovery, Ep4=Revelation, Ep5=Decision, Ep6=Reversal. No repeat types in adjacent episodes.
- **The antagonist must appear before Ep5.** Crowe's presence in Ep4 is physical and specific; he is not a shadow.
- **June Bell's death is the only death of a named character through Ep6.** Do not add more casualties until they are fully earned.
- **Lena Ortiz must not be revealed as a villain.** The "ally who looks like an antagonist" is the series' central structural trick. Do not collapse it early or betray it in the finale by making her complicit.
- **The vault's contents must be discovered by Mara — not handed to her.** She must do the work of understanding what Claire found, not simply receive it.

# ET-COS Book V — The Series Doctrine
**Version:** 1.0 · Founding Edition
**Status:** DRAFT for Marc review
**Authority:** This Book owns series structure, episode arc design, cliffhanger standards, and franchise criteria.

---

## Chapter 1 — Why Series, Not Standalones

A listener who finishes an Endless Tales standalone has had one experience.
A listener who finishes Episode 1 of a 5-episode series is already invested in 4 more.

Series are the primary vehicle for listener loyalty.

**The math is simple:**
- A standalone: one listen, one decision to return
- A 5-episode series: one hook, five automatic listens (if Episode 1 works)

Standalones serve a purpose — they are accessible entry points, they introduce new genres, and they let listeners sample Endless Tales with no commitment. But they are not the retention engine. Series are.

**Catalog target:** At any given time, series should represent at least 60% of active production.

## Chapter 2 — Series Structure Rules

### Minimum Requirements

Every series must have, before production begins:
- A logline (one sentence, present tense, names the protagonist and the situation)
- A central mystery or conflict that cannot be resolved until the finale
- A protagonist with an irreversible wound or situation
- An antagonist (person, force, or circumstance) that escalates across episodes
- A defined finale — what "resolved" looks like for this series

### Episode Count Standards

| Series Type | Episodes | Use Case |
|------------|---------|---------|
| Mini-series | 3 episodes | Self-contained story, fast production, low commitment |
| Standard series | 5–7 episodes | Core catalog format |
| Extended series | 8–12 episodes | Franchise stories, proven characters only |
| Anthology | Unlimited | Shared world, rotating cast — each episode semi-standalone |

Anthology is the exception — each episode must still deliver the Listener Promise independently (Book I, Chapter 4).

## Chapter 3 — The Cliffhanger Standard

Every non-finale episode ends with a cliffhanger. This is not a suggestion.

**Cliffhanger types (from strongest to weakest):**

| Type | Definition | Example |
|------|------------|---------|
| Reversal | Something the listener believed is revealed to be false | The person helping them is the villain |
| Revelation | New information that reframes everything | The victim is still alive |
| Escalation | The threat gets significantly worse | They find a second body |
| Question | An unanswered question with clear stakes | She opens the envelope and goes pale |
| Threat | A direct, named threat to someone the listener cares about | "I know where your daughter goes to school" |

**Cliffhanger Strength scoring (Book II, Genome dim. 13):**

| Score | Meaning |
|-------|---------|
| 9–10 | Listener would not stop listening even if they arrived at their destination |
| 7–8 | Strong pull; most listeners start Episode N+1 within 24 hours |
| 5–6 | Adequate; listeners who are invested will continue |
| 3–4 | Weak; completion depends on overall quality, not the cliffhanger |
| 1–2 | No real cliffhanger; effectively a standalone ending |

**Minimum acceptable score:** 7. A score below 7 triggers a script repair request before production.

## Chapter 4 — Episode Arc Design

Every episode follows a five-beat arc:

| Beat | Name | What Happens |
|------|------|-------------|
| 1 | Hook | The listener is locked in within the first 60-90 seconds |
| 2 | Setup | The situation is established; stakes are made concrete |
| 3 | Rising Action | The protagonist pursues their want; obstacles increase |
| 4 | Mid-Turn | Something changes that makes the situation worse |
| 5 | Escalation + Cliffhanger | The stakes reach their episode peak; the next question is posed |

**For finale episodes, the arc extends:**

| Beat | Name | What Happens |
|------|------|-------------|
| 1-4 | Standard arc | Same as above |
| 5 | Climax | The central conflict reaches its breaking point |
| 6 | Resolution | The situation is resolved — but not necessarily happily |
| 7 | Emotional Landing | The listener feels the weight of what happened |

A finale that ends on a cliffhanger pointing to a new series is acceptable — but the current series' central question must be answered.

## Chapter 5 — The Series Approval Gate (HARD RULE)

**A series may enter Ready for Review ONLY when ALL episodes are fully complete.**

Required for every episode before the series may move:
- Final script and prose ✅
- Voice and audio generation complete ✅
- Belle B intro and outro complete ✅
- Cover art set ✅
- Duration metadata present ✅
- No episode blocked or pending ✅

**If even one episode is incomplete — the entire series stays in production.**

This rule cannot be overridden except by Marc's explicit instruction for a specific story.

## Chapter 6 — Series Continuity Requirements

### What Must Carry Forward

| Element | Rule |
|---------|------|
| Character wounds | Cannot be healed between episodes without scene-level justification |
| Character knowledge | A character cannot un-know something they discovered |
| Physical consequences | Injuries, losses, and revelations persist |
| Introduced antagonists | Cannot disappear without narrative explanation |
| Named locations | Geography and named settings remain consistent |

### The Continuity Log

Every series beyond Episode 2 should have a continuity note (stored in the series Bible or `series.description`) that tracks: who knows what, who is where, and what changed in the previous episode.

ASC is responsible for populating this from the script at the end of each production step.

## Chapter 7 — Series World Design

A series with a rich, specific world retains listeners better than a story-by-story procedural.

**World design requirements for extended series (8+ episodes):**

1. At least 3 named recurring locations
2. At least 2 named recurring characters other than the protagonist
3. A social or physical environment that creates natural conflict (a community with secrets, a institution with rules, a geography with dangers)
4. A mythology or history that the protagonist does not fully understand at the start

**World design is optional for mini-series and standard series** — but teams who build it see consistently higher franchise potential scores.

---

## Chapter 8 — Anthology Rules

An anthology series uses a shared world with rotating characters.

**Anthology requirements:**
- Every episode delivers the Listener Promise independently
- Cliffhanger requirement is reduced (score ≥ 5 acceptable if world intrigue compensates)
- At least one recurring world element connects episodes (a place, a legend, a name)
- Anthology episodes may introduce franchise candidates who appear in later standard series

**Anthology is not a workaround for weak episode endings.** If an episode does not stand on its own, it is not suitable for anthology format.

6. Different setting, different register, higher stakes.

MARA: Yes.

JUNE BELL: I worked the night watch at Camperdown Mill from 1969 to 1985. I was one of eight women on the flood-watch rotation. Women got the night-watch routes because we were cheaper and because the mill superintendent at the time believed we were less likely to drink on the walk. He was right about that last part.

NARRATOR: She set down her glass.

JUNE BELL: The inverted clapper. I saw it used twice. Once in 1974, once in 1979. Both times the same foreman came to us the next morning and told us the route had been rerouted for maintenance. Told us to stamp our tokens normally going forward. The inverted-clapper entries in the ledger would be for administrative purposes only.

MARA: You knew what they meant.

JUNE BELL: We knew. We didn't ask. That was the arrangement. The mill kept things quiet in exchange for keeping the night-watch jobs. Two months' rent was two months' rent.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: The afternoon held them in the sweet-tea quiet. Mara let it hold. Some silences were not pauses; they were payments — and June Bell had been paying interest on this one for fifty years.

I was to bring the mess to specific bags he provided and leave those bags at a specific location.

JUNE BELL: Papers. Documents in boxes, some of them damp from what I assumed was river-adjacent moisture. Photographs. An old cassette recorder that had been smashed. The kind of smashing you do deliberately — not dropped, taken apart. Pieces.

# The Bell Beneath Falls Park — Episode 2: The Seventh Token

## CHARACTER GUIDE

**MARA VANCE** — 35–38, female, Greenville SC accent. Narrator and protagonist. Not a detective — an investigator by necessity, built out of twenty-nine years of unanswered questions. Precise. Steady. The particular stillness of someone who has learned not to show anything until she is certain. Tonight she is holding two versions of her mother's story and they do not both fit.

**DETECTIVE LENA ORTIZ** — mid-40s, female, South Carolina. Close-cropped hair. Trained stillness — the quality of someone who can stand in the dark without flinching. Tactical intelligence operating under partial information. She knows something about the department she has not said.

**ELI MERCER** — mid-40s, male, Southern academic. Local historian. Warm, careful, and carrying more than he has offered. His scholarship is genuine. His full motive is not yet settled.

**JUNE BELL** — early 70s, female, Greenville. Former local investigator. Helped Claire Vance in the years before the hotel fire. Physically unharmed but permanently changed by what she spent the evening knowing. She has been waiting to say one thing for three weeks.

**UNKNOWN MAN** — calm, Southern, deliberate. Not the monster Mara expected. A man sitting in a chair the way someone sits when they have been carrying something for a very long time and have decided to set part of it down.

Come back to Room two seventeen by ten o'clock. Bring the token. Don't call the police.

## MERGE POINT
*(Both openings arrive here — same scene, same moment. All listeners continue together.)*

[SFX: DISTANT GREENVILLE TRAFFIC — INSECTS — THE HUMID WEIGHT OF AUGUST NIGHT AIR]

LENA: Nobody moves until I say so.

[SFX: LENA'S RADIO LIFTING TOWARD HER MOUTH — THEN LOWERING WITHOUT KEYING]

MARA: She didn't call it in. That choice landed in the alley the same way silence lands after a gunshot — shaped, significant, telling you more than the sound itself.

LENA: I have one person in this city I trust completely. One. I'm going to call her and tell her where I'm going tonight and why. She won't dispatch — she'll just know. If I don't check in by midnight, she moves.

MARA: And the rest of your department?

LENA: Not tonight.

ELI: You believe someone in Greenville PD is connected to the tunnel.

LENA: I believe Adrian Cross sent an anonymous tip to pull me into that tunnel at a specific time. Someone knew the channel to use. That is a very short list of people, and I am not announcing my location on a department radio until I understand who's on it.

[BEAT]

MARA: I turned to Eli. We had eighty-nine minutes and I was going to use every one of them.

MARA: How did you know my mother had a flood token.

ELI: I found a note. In the Camperdown Mill flood-watch records — the unofficial ledger, not the public archive. I was researching the mill's demolition history in 2018 when I found a discrepancy: six names in the 1997 flood-watch log that have no other records. No tax filings, no death certificates, nothing after June of that year. I was examining the spine of the ledger when I found a folded piece of paper tucked into the binding.

MARA: What did it say?

ELI: He took out his phone and turned it toward me. The image was a researcher's snapshot — slightly overexposed, taken in natural light. A folded piece of paper. My mother's handwriting. I recognized her block letters the same way you recognize a voice before you hear any words.

MARA: I read it aloud.

MARA: "If you found this in the flood records, you've found what I found. June Bell knows the first checkpoint. Do not go to the police. The end of the route is under the Palmetto Hotel. What I found there is why they need me gone."

[BEAT — 3s]

LENA: She wrote that before she disappeared.

ELI: The paper degraded consistent with the late nineties. I had it tested independently.

LENA: You had it tested and you didn't call this in.

ELI: The note said not to. And Adrian Cross contacted me in March. He said the people who administered the tunnel in 1997 — some are still in Greenville. Active. The reason nothing was ever investigated, he said, was not incompetence. It was architecture.

[SFX: A DISTANT SIREN — MOVING AWAY — THEN NIGHT INSECTS RETURNING]

MARA: June 18, 1997. The date written across Adrian's palm. Tell me what happened that night.

ELI: Official river inspection log shows clear weather, standard levels, routine sign-off. But the unofficial Camperdown night-watch ledger lists three names at Checkpoint One, signed in at 11:42 PM. None of them signed out.

MARA: Names?

ELI: Two I've traced. One worked for the city for fifteen more years. One died in 2003. The third is listed as initials only — D.V. — and beside the initials, someone drew the flood-token symbol. But right-side up. Not inverted the way the tokens are struck.

[LONG PAUSE]

MARA: Lena was already moving toward her car.

LENA: We have eighty-six minutes. Get in.

[SFX: CAR DOORS CLOSING — ENGINE STARTING — THE CITY SLIDING PAST WINDOWS IN THE DARK]

MARA: She drove east, away from the Palmetto Hotel, two miles to a parking structure on Academy Street where the upper deck was empty and the sightlines were clean. She made her one call in four sentences, then holstered the phone.

MARA: I looked at the photograph of Claire's note again. Read it the way you read a thing when you need it to mean something different than it means. It kept meaning the same thing.

MARA: The end of the route is under the Palmetto Hotel.

MARA: Not in Room two seventeen. Under it.

[BEAT]

ELI: When we were in the room earlier — before the gunshot — I picked up the carbon copy form that was beneath the cassette recorder.

[BEAT]

LENA: I know. Hand it over.

[SFX: PAPER UNFOLDING IN THE CLOSE SPACE OF A CAR — DRY AND DELIBERATE]

LENA: Pre-printed ledger form. Old. Six checkpoint entries in a left column, monthly dates from — April 1987 through September 1997.

MARA: They ran it for ten years.

LENA: The last entry is June 18, 1997. One name in the entry column — crossed out. And at the bottom, stamped in red ink. Faded. It says — "ARRIVED."

[LONG BEAT]

ELI: Not a departure. An arrival.

MARA: Someone used the route that night. Not to disappear — to come through. From the western entry point, all six checkpoints, to the end. Under the Palmetto Hotel.

LENA: And three months later, the route was sealed.

[SFX: THE CAR VERY QUIET — CITY SOUNDS FAR AWAY — THEN QUIET AGAIN]

MARA: I sat with that for the rest of the drive. I had been building the architecture of my mother's disappearance for twenty-nine years — every version of it had the same foundation: she was taken. Removed by force. By someone who needed her gone. The new architecture had a different foundation and I had to decide whether it held.

MARA: An escape means she chose. A route means she planned. A cassette recorder in a hidden room beneath the park means she had already decided her daughter might someday come looking, and she wanted to be findable if things were ever safer.

MARA: It means she intended to be gone.

MARA: I sat with that. I did not resolve it. We had seventy-two minutes.

[SFX: ENGINE OFF — DOOR HANDLES — THE THREE OF THEM STEPPING ONTO ASPHALT IN THE DARK]

LENA: Here's how this runs. I go in first through the roof access — I mapped the utility stairwell when we were there before. Third floor down through the center shaft, twenty feet from Room two seventeen. I'll be inside before you arrive. You walk in through the alley door as directed. Eli leads. Mara stays one step behind.

MARA: And when he asks for the token?

LENA: You hand it over. You do not negotiate. You let him speak. And if anything changes shape quickly, I come through the door.

ELI: There's one more thing. The token Adrian was carrying — I read the full edge engraving in the car. It doesn't only say "Room two seventeen." It says: "217 / C2 / 1.19.88."

[BEAT]

MARA: C2.

ELI: Checkpoint two. And a date — January 19, 1988. This token doesn't mark the beginning of the route. It marks the second stop. We've been treating it as the starting point because that's where Adrian was found. But we haven't found Checkpoint One.

[LONG BEAT]

LENA: We started in the middle of something.

MARA: We always were.

[SFX: THREE SETS OF FOOTSTEPS SEPARATING — TWO MOVING TOWARD THE BUILDING — ONE MOVING TOWARD THE ROOFLINE]

---

[SFX: METAL SERVICE DOOR UNLOCKING IN A BACK ALLEY — 9:53PM]

[SFX: THE SAME STAIRCASE — WET PLASTER AND OLD SMOKE — BUT WITH A DIFFERENT QUALITY NOW. OCCUPIED AIR. THE FAINT WARMTH OF A SPACE THAT HAS RECENTLY HELD BREATH.]

MARA: The building had changed. Not the smell, not the architecture. The weight of attention inside it — the quality that a building holds when it has been waiting for something specific to arrive.

MARA: We climbed.

[SFX: TWO PAIRS OF FOOTSTEPS ON STAIRS — CAREFUL, DELIBERATE — ASCENDING]

MARA: The fire door on the second floor stood open. Room two seventeen — no number plate, painted-over outline, the condemnation notice worn pale. The door was open.

MARA: The six tokens were on the table where we had left them.

MARA: And at the center of the circle — a seventh.

[SFX: A SINGLE BRASS COIN PLACED ON A WOODEN TABLE — DELIBERATE — THE SMALL PRECISE WEIGHT OF IT]

MARA: Someone had added it while we were gone.

[BEAT]

ELI: (Quietly) Seven.

MARA: The room held one other change. The cassette recorder was gone. In its place: a wooden chair facing the door. And in the chair — a man.

MARA: He was not threatening in the way I had prepared to be threatened. He sat the way a very tired person sits — elbows on knees, looking at the floor the way people look at floors when they are deciding whether to say something they have been not saying for a long time. His face was in shadow and he did not move it toward the light.

UNKNOWN MAN: You brought Eli.

MARA: We brought what you asked for.

UNKNOWN MAN: I can see the detective in the utility stairwell on the thermal. Tell her she can come in. It doesn't change what I'm going to say.

[SFX: LENA'S CONTROLLED FOOTSTEPS — ENTERING — THE DOOR BEHIND HER STAYING OPEN]

LENA: June Bell. Where is she.

UNKNOWN MAN: Through the far door. She is unharmed. I'll send her through now.

[SFX: A DOOR AT THE BACK OF THE ROOM OPENING — THEN JUNE BELL'S FOOTSTEPS — SLOW, CAREFUL — AN OLDER WOMAN KEEPING HERSELF VERY STEADY]

MARA: June Bell was seventy-three years old and she was refusing to show how frightened she had been, which was how I knew exactly how frightened she had been. She crossed the room and took my arm in both hands and held it the way people hold things they thought they might not hold again.

MARA: I set Adrian's token on the table.

UNKNOWN MAN: Thank you.

MARA: Who are you.

UNKNOWN MAN: My name is not useful tonight. What matters is what I know, and what I know is this: I did not put Adrian Cross in that tunnel. I did not send Eli the photograph. I did not ring the bell. Someone has been running ahead of you, building the path for your specific arrival, and whoever that is — I do not know them. I only know what they've left behind.

LENA: That's convenient.

UNKNOWN MAN: It's the truth. And the truth I want you to carry out of this room is harder than that. You think you have been investigating what happened to your mother. You haven't been. You have been following a trail that was laid for you to follow. Ask yourself: who needs you to find this now? Why tonight? What does the person who laid this path get when you arrive at the end of it?

[BEAT]

MARA: He stood up slowly, unhurried, and moved toward the back wall.

LENA: You're not leaving.

UNKNOWN MAN: I'm already gone. The people who built the route built its exits too.

[SFX: A SOFT MECHANICAL CLICK IN THE BACK WALL — A CONCEALED PANEL OPENING INWARD — FOOTSTEPS RECEDING INTO THE PASSAGE — THE PANEL CLOSING — FOUR SECONDS TOTAL — THEN SILENCE]

[BEAT]

LENA: There's a panel.

MARA: I know.

MARA: He had left the seventh token in the center of the table. I picked it up. Eli was already beside me, already looking at it.

ELI: This one is different.

MARA: I felt it before he said it. Same weight. Same size. Same worn brass edges. But where every other token in the room showed the bell inverted — the striker hanging wrong, the way flood-watch tokens were struck to mark a cleared checkpoint — this one showed the bell upright. Correct. The way a bell looks when it is about to ring.

ELI: The edge engraving. It says — "BELOW / 18 JUN 97 / ASK ADRIAN."

[LONG BEAT — 4s]

MARA: Adrian is dead.

JUNE BELL: (Quietly) He came to see me. Three weeks before he died.

[SFX: EVERYONE STILLING — JUNE'S VOICE LOW AND VERY STEADY IN THE QUIET ROOM]

MARA: I looked at June Bell.

JUNE BELL: He'd been into the tunnel. Below the sixth checkpoint — the end of the route, under this building. He'd found something still intact down there. A record book. The complete log of every transit through the route from 1987 to 1997. He said the last entry was June 18, 1997. Not a departure. An arrival.

MARA: One person.

JUNE BELL: No name. No fee paid. Coming through from the western entry — the beginning of the route, beneath Falls Park — all the way to the end. Adrian said whoever it was, they moved fast. Like they were running from something. Or toward something that was waiting.

[PAUSE]

MARA: My mother's date.

JUNE BELL: Yes.

[LONG BEAT]

MARA: She made it through.

JUNE BELL: That's what the record says.

MARA: Then where is she.

[BEAT — NO ONE ANSWERS]

JUNE BELL: Adrian didn't know. He said the log ends on June 18. But the room below this building is still there — sealed, but structurally intact. He said whatever happened after she arrived, it happened in that room. Whatever she found at the end of the route. It's still there.

[SFX: FROM DIRECTLY BENEATH THEIR FEET — DEEP, STRUCTURAL, MUFFLED BY THIRTY YEARS OF STONE AND SEALED FLOOR — A SMALL HAND BELL. STRUCK ONCE. ONE CLEAN STRIKE. THEN ABSOLUTE SILENCE.]

[BEAT — 4s]

ELI: There is something below this building.

MARA: I know.

[SFX: LENA'S RADIO CRACKLING TO LIFE — URGENT, FRAGMENTED — THEN A VOICE IN STATIC]

LENA: (Into radio, controlled) Say again. — Copy. Don't move, I'm coming to you. — (To the room) Mara.

[PAUSE]

LENA: The officer I left with Adrian Cross's body. He is not answering his radio.

[BEAT]

LENA: We need to move. Now.

[SFX: FOOTSTEPS — ORGANIZED, URGENT — BEGINNING TO MOVE]

[SFX: THE REEDY RIVER — DISTANT — PATIENT — BENEATH THE HOTEL, BENEATH THE CITY, BENEATH EVERY ANSWER THAT HAS NOT YET BEEN FOUND — 5s — THEN THE DISTANT, MUFFLED, IMPOSSIBLE SOUND OF A BELL BELOW THE FLOOR, STRIKING AGAIN — ONCE — BEFORE THE EPISODE ENDS]

[END AUDIO DRAMA SCRIPT]

## PURPOSE

The Story Resolution Map is the required pre-draft structure for every Endless Tales story and episode.

Its job is to stop weak endings before they are written. The map forces the story to define what the listener is waiting to see resolved, why the answer is difficult, and how the middle creates the ending.

## REQUIRED MAP FIELDS

Every story must define these fields before drafting:

### 1. Main hook/problem

The central problem that pulls the listener forward.

It must be concrete, active, and specific.

Good:

- A dispatcher receives calls from a highway that no longer exists.
- A bail bondsman has six hours to find a vanished witness before a guilty man walks.
- A lighthouse keeper sees distress signals from a ship that sank forty years ago.

Weak:

- A town has secrets.
- A woman faces her past.
- A detective investigates a mystery.

### 2. Why the solution seems difficult or impossible

The story must define why the obvious fix will not work.

Examples:

- The only witness refuses to speak because the killer saved her life once.
- The bridge cannot be crossed because the flood took out both roads.
- The evidence exists only on a tape that plays a new version every time.

### 3. Middle escalation path

The middle must increase pressure and narrow options.

It should include:

- A discovery that changes the problem.
- A cost paid by the protagonist.
- A complication caused by the antagonist, environment, or moral stakes.
- A setup that can later become part of the solution.

### 4. Final solution

The final solution must be a specific action, choice, discovery, confrontation, sacrifice, or consequence.

Weak:

- The mystery is solved.
- The hero wins.
- The truth comes out.

Strong:

- Mara broadcasts the corrupted confession through the old fire tower because only analog radio can bypass the mayor's network.
- Hale proves the grave was moved by matching the mine dust on the judge's boots to the sealed tunnel.

### 5. Why the ending is earned

The map must explain why the ending follows from prior setup.

It should identify:

- The seed or clue introduced earlier.
- The decision or sacrifice that makes the solution possible.
- The emotional or moral reason the ending satisfies.

---

## DIFFICULT SOLUTION RULE

The solution must not be easy.

The listener should feel that the protagonist reaches the ending because the story forced them through pressure, not because the plot gave them a shortcut.

Valid difficulty sources:

- Physical danger
- Moral cost
- Time pressure
- Social risk
- Emotional vulnerability
- Contradictory clues
- A solution that requires giving something up

Invalid shortcut sources:

- Sudden coincidence
- New evidence with no setup
- Convenient confession
- Offscreen rescue
- A technology or supernatural rule introduced only at the end

---

## MAP ACCEPTANCE CHECK

A Story Resolution Map is acceptable only if all answers are specific enough that a validator or editor can tell whether the finished script fulfilled them.

Fail the map if:

- The hook is generic.
- The difficulty is not stated.
- The middle escalation does not affect the ending.
- The solution could happen in minute two without the story changing.
- The earned-ending explanation uses vague language such as "because of the journey."

## EXAMPLES

### Strong map

Main hook/problem: A night-shift toll collector receives exact change from drivers who died on that road years ago.

Why difficult: The toll booth records prove the cars never arrived, and if she leaves the booth before sunrise, the only living witness disappears.

Middle escalation: She discovers the dead drivers all paid with coins minted the year of a bridge collapse, then learns her father approved the unsafe repair that caused it.

Final solution: She keeps the booth open long enough to record every dead driver's name, then broadcasts the list over the emergency channel before the county can erase the logs.

Why earned: The coins, booth recorder, and emergency channel are all established before the ending; the protagonist must choose public truth over protecting her father's name.

### Weak map

Main hook/problem: A toll booth is haunted.

Why difficult: It is scary.

Middle escalation: Things get worse.

Final solution: The truth is revealed.

Why earned: The story builds to it.

This fails because it does not define pressure, causality, or a real ending.

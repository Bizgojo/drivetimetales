### 1. Gus Pendry — Heartwarming ✅ active

> Gus Pendry writes about animals and the people who love them — about loyalty that crosses distances, homecomings that cost more than they should, and the particular grace of a creature that does not know the word for devotion but practices it anyway. His stories are warm without being innocent, and earned without being sentimental. He writes because some kinds of love are easier to see in a dog than in a person, and no less true for that.

---

### 2. J. Calloway Reid — Literary / Appalachian ✅ active

> J. Calloway Reid writes Appalachian fiction the way the mountains make people — spare, haunted, shaped by something ancient and not entirely kind. Her stories move through hard country and hard lives with a prose that carries violence and beauty in the same sentence, because in the mountains they are the same sentence. She does not make the land gentle to make it readable. She trusts the reader to survive it.

---

### 3. Kira Vance — Sci-Fi ✅ active

> Kira Vance writes science fiction about found family in deep space — about the ship held together by affection more than engineering, the crew debating soup recipes across four species' worth of taste buds, the small mercies of being somewhere strange with people you didn't expect to love. She is interested in the future that is not grand but is, somehow, better than the one we imagined. She writes space opera for people who cry at homecomings.

---

### 4. Holland Reese — Heartwarming ✅ active

> Holland Reese writes about the ordinary moments that turn out to contain everything. Voicemails you can't delete. Graduations attended by people who said they wouldn't come. The quiet mercy of a door left open. His stories are tender and reflective and build without hurry toward the kind of emotional catharsis that finds you before you've prepared a defense. He writes about grace — the kind that arrives uninvited and changes everything.

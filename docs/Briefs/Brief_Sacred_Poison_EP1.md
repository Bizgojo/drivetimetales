## PART 3 — STORY CONTENT

**The Core Premise:**

Rosa Delgado has cleaned the Asheville Catholic Basilica for twenty-three years. On a Sunday morning in early spring, after the 10 AM mass ends and the congregation files out, she descends to the basement to polish the old stone floors as she does every week. Instead, she finds three bodies sprawled in the wine cellar — Father Matthias who gave the sermon just hours before, a parishioner named David Torrence, and a young woman Rosa has never seen. All three show signs of poisoning. Rosa's scream echoes through the empty basilica and sets off a chain of events that will expose secrets no one in the church was meant to uncover.

Detective James Ward arrives to find a sanctuary turned crime scene. His investigation immediately points to something deliberate, premeditated — but why the basilica? Why Sunday morning? And who is the unknown woman?

**The Setting:**

Asheville, North Carolina, present day. Early spring. The Asheville Catholic Basilica is a beautiful, Gothic-style stone building built in 1910, located in downtown Asheville. Underground wine cellar with original brick arches. The detective bureau downtown. Streets of downtown Asheville, North Carolina. The basilica's main sanctuary, the sacristy, the parish offices. A community where people know each other.

**Specific Story Requirements:**

- This is a mystery. Rosa discovers the bodies. Keep the focus on the discovery and initial shock.
- Detective Ward arrives and begins the investigation.
- End on a strong cliffhanger that raises questions: Who is the woman? Why were these people together? Who put them there?
- No graphic gore — imply the poisoning through symptoms and behavior rather than describe it in detail.
- The church setting is important. Treat the basilica as a character — its weight, its history, its sanctity violated.
- Include the old fiddle player's reaction or knowledge — he sits outside the basilica and notices things.
- Music should feel mournful, violated, like something sacred has been defiled.

## PART 4 — AUDIO & MUSIC DIRECTION

**Overall Music Energy:**

Mournful and atmospheric — church organ undertones, something sacred gone wrong. Cold, reverent, deeply unsettled.

**Music Reference:**

True Detective Season 1 opening theme, the TV show "Mindhunter," Hans Zimmer's "Interstellar" (the unsettling parts)

**Key Dramatic Moments Needing Music Shifts:**

- Opening — Rosa enters the basement, casual routine, ordinary. Music is almost not there, just texture.
- The moment she discovers the bodies — music drops out. Silence. Just her breathing, her footsteps, her scream.
- When Detective Ward arrives and surveys the scene — darker, heavier, the weight of what's happened settles in. Organ drone underneath.
- The old fiddle player recognizing one of the dead — music shifts to something knowing, dark, portentous.
- Closing cliffhanger — music rises, questions unanswered, listener left in dread.

**SFX Density:** standard

**SFX Priority Environments:**

- The basilica basement — cold stone, echoing, the wine cellar's weight
- The sanctuary — vast, quiet, then violated
- Downtown Asheville streets — morning traffic, church bells
- Detective bureau — phones, fluorescent hum, papers

**Audio Tone Notes:**

This is a moment of violation. Treat the audio as if something sacred has been profaned. The basilica sounds should be warm and holy in Rosa's early scenes, then cold and wrong when the bodies are discovered. The shift in music and SFX atmosphere should make the listener feel the violation as sharply as Rosa does.

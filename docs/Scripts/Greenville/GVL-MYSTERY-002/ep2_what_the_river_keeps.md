[START AUDIO DRAMA SCRIPT]

NARRATOR: Day two began where day one gave up — on the water. Corinne Shaw had been missing for twenty-six hours. She'd gone into the mile-six tunnel at an eight-forty pace and never come out, and the county had decided that meant the river took her. So at first light on Wednesday, they searched the river.

NARRATOR: Ivy Shaw walked the wetland boardwalks north of Furman with the volunteer line, still awake from a shift she'd never finished, calling her sister's name into the reeds.

NARRATOR: The boards knocked under forty pairs of boots. Nothing answered but the marsh.

NARRATOR: Ivy had been awake for thirty hours. She knew exactly what that did to judgment — she'd sent nurses home for less — and she kept walking anyway, because the alternative was sitting in her car with her sister's spare key in her pocket, thinking about Tuesday dinner. The volunteers were kind to her in the useless way people are kind at searches. Nobody would say the word body, and everybody was looking for one.

NARRATOR: By eight, the divers were working the pools below the old trestles, and Ivy stood on the rail bridge above them, watching bubbles rise through brown water.

NARRATOR: They brought up a shopping cart, half a dock ladder, and a truck tire from nineteen-something. They did not bring up Corinne, because Corinne wasn't in the river, and by noon even the deputy running the grid had started saying so with his shoulders.

NARRATOR: Nate Okafor spent that same morning in the trailhead lot with a laptop on his tailgate, doing the only thing he knew how to do for her. He pulled six years of club ride files — every GPS track any member had ever recorded through the mile-six tunnel — and laid Corinne's last run on top of them. Eleven thousand passes through that tunnel. Eleven thousand tracks going in one end and out the other.

NARRATOR: And one that didn't.

NATE OKAFOR: Ivy. Come look at this. Please.

NARRATOR: Ivy came off the boardwalk with mud to her knees. Nate turned the screen toward her and kept his voice level, which cost him.

NATE OKAFOR: Every line here goes through. Forty yards, four seconds for a bike, twelve for a runner. Hers goes in twenty meters and ends. No exit ping north, no exit ping south. As far as the satellites are concerned, your sister left that tunnel through a wall.

IVY SHAW: Walls don't take people, Nate.

NATE OKAFOR: No. And here's the other thing. Her watch has an accelerometer — it feels motion even without signal. The last two seconds of her file aren't a stumble. Cadence cuts from a dead sprint to zero, one hard lateral spike, then the watch goes stationary on the ground.

[BEAT]

NATE OKAFOR: A fall tapers. This doesn't taper. Her file doesn't end like a fall. It ends like a hand.

NARRATOR: Ivy looked at the little broken line on the screen for a long moment, and when she spoke, her voice was quieter and harder than it had been all day.

IVY SHAW: Then somebody was in that tunnel with her. And somebody knows where she is right now.

NARRATOR: Nate took the overlay to the deputy running the search. He walked him through it twice — eleven thousand clean passes, one broken line, the cadence spike. The deputy studied the screen the way people study things they've decided not to understand, and said the data would be forwarded. Nate came back to the tailgate and closed the laptop with more care than it needed.

NATE OKAFOR: Forwarded. Eleven thousand to one, and they're going to forward it.

IVY SHAW: Then we stop asking them to see it. We find what she found.

NARRATOR: Grady Polk had been waiting at the edge of the lot for twenty minutes, hat in his hands, working up to something. He'd shown the sheriff the pry-marks at dawn. The sheriff had allowed that kids get into everything and asked him to keep the trail gates locked. Grady had driven back down the closed trail with his jaw set, and now he came up to Nate's tailgate and put his finger on the laptop screen, on the blank space between the trail and the river.

GRADY POLK: Your map's wrong, son. Not your fault. Every map of this corridor's been wrong for a hundred years.

NATE OKAFOR: Wrong how?

GRADY POLK: Nineteen twenty-two, the Greenville and Northern ran a service bore under this grade. Steam line first, water line after. Brick and concrete, tall enough to walk bent over. It runs under the trail, under the road at mile six. Railroad died, county sealed both ends, and everybody who remembered it is in the ground. Except me.

IVY SHAW: You're telling me there's a second tunnel under my sister's tunnel.

GRADY POLK: I'm telling you there's a grate fifteen feet from where her shoe was found, with fresh pry-marks on it and new bolts turned from the inside. I'm telling you the sheriff called it raccoons.

[BEAT]

GRADY POLK: Raccoons don't torque half-inch steel, ma'am.

NARRATOR: He went to his truck and came back with something flat wrapped in an old feed sack. He unwrapped it on the tailgate next to Nate's laptop like the two of them were finally being introduced. A linen map, hand-inked, brittle at the folds. Greenville and Northern Railway, valuation survey, nineteen twenty-two. And there, drawn in a draftsman's careful red — a second line running under the grade, under the road at mile six, down along the river.

GRADY POLK: Granddaddy's copy. Railroad's dead, county's got no record, but the bore never went anywhere. Concrete don't forget just because paper does.

NATE OKAFOR: This runs — what, two miles? Where does it end?

GRADY POLK: North end's up toward Travelers Rest, in the wooded cut. South end daylights at the riverbank, below the grade. Culvert mouth, right at the water.

NARRATOR: It was Ivy who found the why. She'd been carrying Corinne's tablet in her bag for two days — the sheriff had Corinne's phone, but the photos synced to the cloud, and the cloud didn't need a warrant from a sister who knew the passcode. She opened the camera roll in the front seat of Grady's truck, and there was Monday evening, the last golden hour before the tunnel, laid out in forty-one photographs.

NARRATOR: Survey stakes with orange flagging, driven in a line through protected woods. A core-drill rig on a flatbed, tucked behind brush off the trail shoulder near Travelers Rest. Fresh cracks crazing the asphalt of the trail bed, photographed with Corinne's shoe in frame for scale. A chain-link fence cut and folded back. And in the last nine photos, shot long-lens through the trees — a concrete portal in the wooded cut, standing open, with a man in a high-visibility jacket at the mouth of it.

NARRATOR: The second-to-last photo was of a paper card zip-tied to the fence. A utility locate ticket. Ivy spread her fingers on the screen and read the signature out loud.

IVY SHAW: R. Dutton. Ridgeline Site Services.

[BEAT]

IVY SHAW: She told Nate she had pictures. She told me she was bringing me something Tuesday night. This is it. This is what she found.

NARRATOR: They drove north to Travelers Rest with the tablet on Ivy's lap. They didn't have to search for the spot. A half-mile out, with the windows down, they heard it.

NARRATOR: Machinery, running hard, somewhere back in the trees off the protected right-of-way. Not starting a job. Finishing one. Through the fence line they could see the flatbed being loaded, stakes coming up, a man walking the line pulling orange flagging down as fast as his hands could move.

NARRATOR: Nate photographed all of it through the fence with his phone — the plates on the flatbed, the drill rig, the man with the flagging — while Ivy held Corinne's tablet up beside the scene like a before-and-after. Monday evening, stakes going in. Wednesday afternoon, stakes coming out. In between, a woman had vanished off the face of the trail.

IVY SHAW: They're erasing it. Two more hours and there was never anything here.

NARRATOR: Grady watched for one full minute. Then he explained what the county had refused to hear all day, in the voice of a man reading a verdict.

GRADY POLK: That development east of the trail needs sewer and fiber run under this corridor. Protected right-of-way. Easement review'd take 'em eighteen months, and it might come back no. Unless the passage under the trail already exists. Unless somebody quietly opens my granddaddy's dead bore and runs their conduit through a tunnel no map remembers.

NATE OKAFOR: And Monday evening a woman with a camera photographed the portal standing open, the ticket, the man. And Tuesday at six-eleven she ran into the one place their bore touches the trail.

GRADY POLK: The mile-six grate. Somebody was at it when she came through.

[BEAT]

IVY SHAW: Then she's not in the river. She's under the trail. She's been under our feet the whole time.

NARRATOR: The sheriff listened differently with forty-one photographs on his desk, Nate's plate shots, Grady's linen map, and a locate ticket bearing a name. He said the words obstruction and right-of-way and, finally, abduction, and for the first time in two days somebody with a badge looked frightened on Corinne's behalf. Then he said the other words. Warrants take what warrants take — Ridgeline's yard, the bore, Dutton — first thing in the morning, and not one minute sooner.

IVY SHAW: She's been under the ground since Tuesday. She doesn't have your paperwork's patience.

NARRATOR: The sheriff said morning again, gently, the way people say things they can't fix. Ivy walked out of the substation at ten that night and stood in the parking lot and knew, the way she knew her sister's pace, that morning was too far away.

NARRATOR: She didn't ask Grady and Nate to come. She didn't have to. Grady's truck rolled up the closed trail at eleven with its lights off, three of them inside, nobody talking.

NARRATOR: They walked the last hundred yards to the mile-six cut in fog thick enough to chew. Grady's flashlight found the cut wall, the kudzu, the old iron frame.

NARRATOR: The grate stood open.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: The new bolts lay in the grass like shell casings. The iron sill of the culvert shone wet in the light.

NARRATOR: Bootprints, fresh enough that the edges still glistened, crossed the sill and went down into the dark. One set. Going down.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: None coming back.

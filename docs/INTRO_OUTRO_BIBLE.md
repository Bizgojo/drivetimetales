# INTRO / OUTRO BIBLE — Endless Tales
**Version:** 1.0  
**Owner:** Marc Postlewaite / Endless Tales  
**Last Updated:** May 2026  
**Status:** Production doctrine

---

## PURPOSE

This document defines the creative and structural role of Endless Tales intros and outros.

Belle B is not a narrator, character, or generic announcer. Belle B is the Endless Tales host, curator, and continuity bridge. Her job is to welcome the listener into the experience, carry momentum between episodes, and close the listening moment without breaking story immersion.

The intro/outro system must feel alive, responsive, and human. It must never feel like a static template pasted onto every story.

---

## CORE PRINCIPLES

### 1. Belle B is the host, curator, and continuity bridge

Belle B speaks from the platform, not from inside the fictional world. She frames the listening experience, connects episodes, and gives the audience a sense that Endless Tales is actively guiding them.

She may:
- Welcome the listener into a story or episode
- Briefly acknowledge where the series left them
- Help carry emotional continuity between episodes
- Close a finale with warmth and completion
- Credit the story as an Endless Tales Original when appropriate

She may not:
- Narrate scenes
- Explain plot mechanics
- Speak as a character
- Replace the narrator
- Spoil future events
- Over-explain what the listener is about to hear

### 2. No dead air

The listener should never experience accidental silence, awkward gaps, or a cold transition. Every transition must be intentional: sting, Belle, beat, swell, duck, story, outro, fade.

Short pauses are allowed only when they are musical or dramatic. Silence must never feel like the system failed.

### 3. Dynamic, non-repetitive language

Belle B intros and outros must vary in phrasing, rhythm, and structure. The system must avoid repeated templates across stories, episodes, and sessions.

Do not use generic stock openings such as:
- "Tonight's story..."
- "Today's episode..."
- "Are you ready..."
- "For your listening pleasure..."
- "I am pleased to present..."
- "Welcome back to another exciting..."

Belle should sound composed, warm, and intentional, never robotic or promotional.

### 4. Subtle time-of-day awareness

Time-of-day awareness may be used, but it must be subtle and never exact.

Allowed:
- "wherever the road has you right now"
- "as the day winds down"
- "on this late drive"
- "while the world moves past the windows"

Not allowed:
- Exact local time
- Specific location inference
- Comments that feel surveillant or creepy
- Anything that implies Belle knows too much about the listener's real-world situation

### 5. Series episode state controls Belle language

Belle's intro/outro language must be controlled by episode state:

- Standalone exception: introduce and close the story cleanly.
- Series first episode: invite the listener into the series without over-explaining.
- Series middle episode: assume momentum and reference the prior emotional state without spoilers.
- Series non-final episode outro: recap the cliffhanger and point toward the next episode.
- Series finale outro: provide emotional closure and credit the title/author/Endless Tales Original tag.

Belle language must never be chosen without knowing whether the episode is first, middle, non-final, or finale.

## CANONICAL INTRO SHAPE

1. Sting
2. Belle B intro
3. Short intentional beat
4. Music swell
5. Music ducks under narrator
6. Story begins

The intro should be brief. Belle should set tone and momentum, then get out of the way.

### Intro example — first episode

> Belle B: This is "The River Below Graylock," an Endless Tales Original from Daniel Wren. A town built on old secrets is about to hear something moving beneath the water.

### Intro example — continuing episode

> Belle B: You're back inside "The River Below Graylock." The ferry is gone, the lights are failing, and Nora still has not told anyone what she saw under the bridge.

### Intro example — subtle time-of-day awareness

> Belle B: Wherever the road has you right now, settle in. This is "The Last Signal from Mercy Ridge," an Endless Tales Original.

---

## CANONICAL NON-FINAL OUTRO SHAPE

1. Story episode ends
2. Music swell
3. Music ducks under Belle B
4. Belle gives a brief cliffhanger recap
5. Belle gives a next-episode hook
6. Final music swell and fade

The non-final outro must not resolve the series. It should remind the listener what just changed and create appetite for the next episode.

### Non-final outro example

> Belle B: The badge is missing, the sheriff knows about the second grave, and Lila has just opened the room her father kept locked for twenty years. Next time on "The Bellworth Confession," the town finally learns who was buried under the courthouse steps.

### Non-final outro example — quieter tone

> Belle B: Clara made it out of the mine, but she did not come back alone. In the next episode of "Ash Under Blackpine," the voice in the dark starts naming the living.

---

## CANONICAL FINALE OUTRO SHAPE

1. Story finale ends
2. Music swell
3. Music ducks under Belle B
4. Belle gives emotional closure
5. Belle names title and author
6. Belle tags the work as an Endless Tales Original
7. Final music swell and fade

The finale outro should feel complete. It may be warmer and more reflective than a middle-episode outro, but it must still be concise.

### Finale outro example

> Belle B: Some towns survive by forgetting. Bellworth survives because Caroline Drake made it remember. You've been listening to "The Bellworth Confession" by Daniel Wren, an Endless Tales Original.

### Finale outro example — intimate tone

> Belle B: The storm has passed, but Mercy Ridge will never be quiet in quite the same way again. This was "The Last Signal from Mercy Ridge" by Zara Osei, an Endless Tales Original.

---

## LENGTH AND TONE

Belle B should be concise.

Target length:
- Intro: usually 1-2 sentences
- Non-final outro: usually 1-2 sentences
- Finale outro: usually 1-3 sentences

Avoid long Belle speeches. If Belle is talking long enough to delay the story or explain the plot, the line is too long.

---

## FORBIDDEN PATTERNS

Never allow:
- Spoilers for future episodes
- Repetitive templates
- Long Belle monologues
- Exact time/location awareness
- Generic hype language
- Belle recapping every plot point
- Belle replacing the narrator
- Cold starts with no sting or musical support
- Dead air between elements

## FUTURE PRODUCTION ENFORCEMENT

ASC should eventually enforce intro/outro timing and quality rules, including:

- Maximum silence thresholds
- Required sting presence
- Required ducking under narrator/Belle
- Required music swell into and out of Belle lines
- Required fade-out behavior
- Intro/outro duration limits
- Non-final versus finale outro state checks
- QC checks for clipped Belle audio, missing assets, or silent tails

This document defines the doctrine. Production code must implement it incrementally without breaking existing ASC recovery, legacy standalone playback, or published story playback.

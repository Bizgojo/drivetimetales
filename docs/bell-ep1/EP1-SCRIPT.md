# The Bell Beneath Falls Park — Episode 1: The Flood Token

**SERIES:** The Bell Beneath Falls Park
**EPISODE:** 001 — EPISODE-001
**EPISODE TITLE:** The Flood Token
**NARRATOR:** Mara Vance
**ANNOUNCER:** Belle B (casting handled separately — not scripted here)

---

## CHARACTER GUIDE

**MARA VANCE** — 35–38, female, Greenville SC accent. Narrator and protagonist. Not a detective — an investigator by necessity, built out of twenty-nine years of unanswered questions. Precise. Steady. The particular stillness of someone who has learned not to show anything until she is certain.

**DETECTIVE LENA ORTIZ** — mid-40s, female, South Carolina. Close-cropped hair. Trained stillness — the quality of someone who can stand in the dark without flinching. Enters crouching over a body like she has done this a hundred times and does not intend to do anything differently now. Knows more than she says.

**ELI MERCER** — mid-40s, male, Southern academic. Local historian. Warm, slightly startled by fieldwork, but comfortable in tunnels in a way that a man who only loves old things probably should not be. He contacted Mara eight weeks ago. He knows things he hasn't explained yet.

**ADRIAN CROSS** — early 60s, male, American Southern (faded). Former investigative journalist. Papery urgency. A man who came to this tunnel to finish one last thing.

**CLAIRE VANCE** — recorded voice. Mid-30s at time of recording. Warm beneath the fear. She prepared carefully for a moment she hoped would never come. The pause she puts before her daughter's name is unmistakable.

**JUNE BELL** — older woman, Greenville. Helped Claire investigate the Palmetto Hotel years ago. Her danger ends this episode.

**UNKNOWN MAN** — calm, Southern, close to the phone. He is not frightened by what he is doing.

---

[START AUDIO DRAMA SCRIPT]

MARA: My name is Mara Vance, and a dead man whispered it after his heart had stopped — beneath Greenville's Falls Park, on the night I finally stopped believing my mother had chosen to leave.

[SFX: REEDY RIVER ROAR — CLOSE AND FORCEFUL, RUSHING BENEATH STONE, 3s]

MARA: In Greenville, the Reedy River keeps its secrets beneath Falls Park, especially after dark. I came because Eli Mercer sent me a photograph: Adrian Cross lying facedown beneath Liberty Bridge, one hand closed around something metallic that caught the light wrong. Adrian had been trying to reach me for two weeks with the claim that had been following me for twenty-nine years — that my mother's disappearance was murder, not abandonment. That night he was finally ready to prove it. His message to Eli said twelve words: "Tell Claire's daughter the bell was never in the church."

MARA: Detective Lena Ortiz had already posted officers at both ends of the bridge. Eli knew a maintenance path behind the old stone wall.

[SFX: TWO SETS OF FOOTSTEPS ON WET STONE, MOVING QUICKLY — WATER DRIPPING AHEAD, LOW ELECTRICAL HUM BENEATH]

ELI: Mara, stay close to me. Lena posted officers at both ends of the bridge — she will arrest us before she hears your explanation.

MARA: Then we don't give her the chance to hear it at the entrance.

MARA: He guided me beneath Liberty Bridge, past floodwater branches left against the concrete supports. The maintenance path ended at an iron door standing half an inch open. The hinge grease was fresh. Someone had been through it recently.

MARA: I told Eli to wait.

MARA: Inside, the tunnel smelled of wet brick and old iron — stone that had absorbed decades of river water and decided to keep it. I pushed the light forward. The water rushed beside the path, close and forceful.

[SFX: METAL DOOR GROAN — THEN A HARD ECHOING SLAM BEHIND HER]

MARA: Adrian Cross lay beside the runoff channel, facedown, one fist closed around something metallic. Water ran dark three inches from his hand.

MARA: I checked his breathing. Nothing. I pressed two fingers to his neck and held them there. Still. His skin held the temperature of the tunnel, not of a living person.

MARA: Then Eli stepped out of the stairwell behind me, breathing hard.

MARA: I told you not to follow.

ELI: You also told me Adrian's message mentioned Claire Vance. I decided your safety outranked your instructions.

MARA: I didn't answer that. I looked at Adrian's closed fist.

MARA: His fingers were loosening — not from life returning, but from the last tension releasing. The brass token slipped free and rang once against the river rock.

[SFX: SMALL METAL OBJECT STRIKING STONE — A SINGLE BRIEF RING]

MARA: I picked it up. A disc the size of a silver dollar, heavier than it looked. On one face, a bell. On the other, a striker — hanging inverted. Wrong.

ELI: Mara. Your mother had a flood token. Exactly like this one.

MARA: Before I could ask him how he knew that, footsteps rang from the far passage — fast, purposeful, certain of the ground.

[SFX: FAST FOOTSTEPS ON WET STONE, APPROACHING FROM DISTANCE — LOW ELECTRICAL HUM PULSES ONCE]

MARA: Detective Lena Ortiz came through the opposite passage, already gloved, flashlight raised. She moved directly to Adrian and crouched over him.

LENA: Do not touch him.

MARA: You knew he was here?

LENA: I received an anonymous call. Same as you, apparently.

MARA: She pressed two fingers to his neck and held them there.

LENA: He has no pulse. Mara, step back.

MARA: She took the token from my hand in a single practiced motion and turned it under her flashlight. Slowly. Edge by edge.

LENA: The edge is engraved. It says — "Room two seventeen."

[BEAT]

ELI: Room two seventeen. The old Palmetto Hotel on Main Street, converted to offices after a fire in nineteen ninety-eight.

MARA: My mother worked there during the final week before she vanished.

LENA: Eli Mercer. Why do you have knowledge of that building's layout?

ELI: The second floor was sealed after the fire. But the alley entrance still opens with a service key. Adrian Cross gave it to me yesterday. He said Mara would need it after the river gave up the bell.

LENA: He gave you a key.

ELI: He said he wouldn't be able to bring her himself.

[BEAT — SILENCE — THEN THE TUNNEL FLOOR SHUDDERS]

MARA: Something struck beneath us — far below the tunnel floor, deeper than the stone should have allowed. Not a tremor. A bell. Single. Massive. Iron-dark. One strike that rose through the rock into our chests and shook a slow curtain of grit down from the brick ceiling above us.

[SFX: SINGLE MASSIVE IRON MILL BELL STRIKE — BONE-DEEP RESONANCE, LONG DECAY — GRIT AND DUST RAINING FROM THE BRICK CEILING ABOVE, 8s]

LENA: There is no bell under Falls Park.

ELI: That is a Camperdown flood token — workers carried them during flood watch on the Reedy River. Each one authenticated a checkpoint along the flood-watch route. When the river rose faster than the sluices could handle, the bell rang. They walked checkpoint to checkpoint and carried the token to prove they'd been there.

MARA: They stopped using these when?

ELI: Before the old mill came down. Thirty years at least.

MARA: Then Adrian Cross opened his eyes.

MARA: The man Lena had just confirmed — no pulse, skin already cold — opened his eyes and turned them toward me. His chest never moved.

ADRIAN: Mara... the bell knows.

MARA: He said it once. Then whatever had briefly returned to him passed back through somewhere else, and he went still again.

MARA: Lena did not lower her flashlight. Eli did not move.

MARA: Adrian's fist was open now. I looked at his palm.

MARA: Across it, in fresh black ink, three words: ASK WHO DROWNED.

MARA: Beneath the words, a date.

MARA: June eighteenth, nineteen ninety-seven. The night my mother vanished.

[BEAT]

LENA: That ink is not more than a few hours old. Someone put that there after he was already down.

[BEAT — THEN A HARD SPLASH FROM THE FAR PASSAGE]

[SFX: HEAVY WATER SPLASH — A PERSON RUNNING HARD INTO STANDING WATER, TUNNEL ECHO]

[SFX: RAPID FOOTSTEPS ON WET STONE, HARD SOLE IMPACTS, EACH STEP DISTINCT, RECEDING FAST]

MARA: Someone was running. From deeper in the tunnel, from the direction of the far passage — already twenty paces ahead and moving fast.

LENA: Greenville Police! Stop!

[SFX: LENA'S FOOTSTEPS PURSUING — HARD, RAPID, MOVING AWAY BENEATH OLD BRICK ARCHES]

MARA: Lena ran beneath the park, past old brick arches and a rusted gate. The fleeing figure cut left toward the service stairs. Lena followed. Eli caught my arm beside a section of wall scratched all over with names — visitors, workers, people who'd found this tunnel and needed to mark that they'd been here.

ELI: Mara, look.

MARA: One name had been carved deeper than the others. The stone dust at the cut edges was still pale, not yet darkened by river damp. Recent work.

MARA: CLAIRE VANCE.

MARA: Below it, a fresh arrow pointing left toward a narrow iron door I had already walked past twice without seeing.

[SFX: SHARP METALLIC LATCH CLICK SNAPPING OPEN — A BRIEF PAUSE — THEN A HEAVY IRON DOOR DRAGGING SLOWLY OVER STONE FLOOR]

MARA: It opened into a hidden room. No larger than a storage closet. Ceiling low enough to make Eli duck. The air inside was dry — sealed from the river damp for years. A wooden chair. A cassette recorder on the stone floor beside it, gray with age. And on the far wall, five photographs pinned beneath a city flood map.

MARA: Adrian. Lena. Eli.

MARA: And me.

[BEAT]

MARA: Someone had built this before Adrian died. These photographs — pinned beneath a map of every place the floodwater ran — prepared in advance. They had known we would all be here tonight.

MARA: Then the cassette clicked on by itself.

[SFX: CASSETTE MECHANISM ENGAGING — BRIEF CLICK — THEN LOW TAPE HISS]

CLAIRE VANCE: Mara, if you ever hear this, one of these people knows why I never came home.

[PAUSE]

CLAIRE VANCE: I do not know which one. I thought I trusted the right person. I understand now that I was wrong somewhere, and I cannot find the place where it broke.

MARA: My mother's voice. Older than the version I had been keeping in my head. But the pause she put before my name — the specific pause Claire Vance placed before anything that mattered to her — that was exactly right. I would have known that pause in any tunnel, in any city.

[SFX: DISTANT FROM DEEP IN THE PASSAGE — LENA'S VOICE, MUFFLED BY STONE AND DISTANCE]

LENA (DISTANT): I have you. Stay down. Stay—

[SFX: SINGLE GUNSHOT — BRICK CHAMBER, TIGHT SLAP-BACK ECHO — THEN ABSOLUTE SILENCE]

[BEAT — 3s]

MARA: The recording continued beneath the echo.

CLAIRE VANCE: Do not trust the person who brings you the bell.

[SFX: CASSETTE STOPPING — MECHANICAL CLICK]

[PAUSE — 3s]

[SFX: SLOW, UNEVEN FOOTSTEPS RETURNING THROUGH THE TUNNEL]

MARA: Lena came back. No one with her. A cut above her left eyebrow. Her weapon holstered. She looked at Eli for a moment before she looked at me.

LENA: The runner got through the service stairs. I fired at a shadow. Nobody was hit.

MARA: There's blood on your sleeve.

LENA: Not mine. I am leaving an officer with the body. Then the three of us are going to Main Street, and both of you stay where I can see you.

[MUSIC: SLOW, LOW — CELLO ONLY, SPARSE]

[SFX: CAR DOORS CLOSING — BRIEF CITY ROAD SOUNDS — THEN SILENCE]

MARA: She drove. Three blocks north. The Palmetto Hotel stood dark above the closed shops on Main Street, its upper windows painted over, its fire-damaged face smoothed by nearly thirty years of weather. A building Greenville had decided to stop looking at.

MARA: My mother had worked on that second floor. One week before she never came home.

[SFX: HUMID NIGHT AIR — METAL SERVICE DOOR UNLOCKING IN A BACK ALLEY]

MARA: Eli opened the alley door. We climbed a staircase that smelled of wet plaster and old smoke. On the second floor, the fire door bore a condemnation notice worn to near-illegibility. Room two seventeen had no number plate — someone had painted over the outline long ago.

LENA: Mara, behind me. Eli — both hands where I can see them.

[SFX: OLD DOOR SWINGING OPEN ON DRY HINGES]

MARA: Lena pushed the door open with her shoulder.

MARA: The windowless room held a folding table. A cassette recorder. A Greenville street map pinned flat across the table surface. And six brass bell tokens arranged in a circle — each one stamped with the same inverted striker.

ELI: That recorder was not here yesterday.

MARA: Masking tape across the cassette. My mother's handwriting — the careful block letters she used when something mattered. One word: MARA.

MARA: I pressed play.

[SFX: CASSETTE BUTTON CLICKING — THEN LOW TAPE HISS]

CLAIRE VANCE: Mara, this is Claire Vance. If you are hearing this, Adrian Cross is dead, and the people who buried the bell have begun removing everyone who knows where it came from.

[PAUSE]

CLAIRE VANCE: The bell beneath Falls Park is not a relic. It is a marker for a tunnel under the Reedy River. That tunnel moved people through Greenville without police records, hospital records, or death certificates. People who needed to disappear used it. And some who disappeared did not choose to.

LENA: Claire Vance is describing an organized disappearance network.

CLAIRE VANCE: There are six tokens. Six checkpoints along the route. I had the first one. The others are still in place — and what I found at the end of that route, what I was going to do with it, is what they needed to stop. Find the others. Follow the route to the end.

[PAUSE]

CLAIRE VANCE: Mara. Do not trust the person who brings you the bell.

[SFX: CASSETTE STOPPING]

[BEAT]

MARA: Eli stared at the token in my hand. He did not move.

ELI: Mara. I did not bring you the bell. Adrian did.

[SFX: GUNSHOT IN THE HALLWAY — SHARP CRACK — GLASS EXPLODING, LIGHT FIXTURE SHATTERING ABOVE LENA — DARKNESS]

LENA: Move — stairwell, now!

[SFX: DOOR IMPACT — THREE SETS OF FOOTSTEPS POUNDING DOWN A NARROW STAIRWELL — URGENT, DESCENDING FAST]

MARA: Lena shoved Eli and me through the doorway. We ran down the stairs — wet plaster, smoke, the weight of the building pressing in on both sides. The alley door hit the wall when we burst through it.

[SFX: ALLEY DOOR SLAMMING OPEN — DISTANT GREENVILLE TRAFFIC — NIGHT INSECTS — THE SUDDEN OPENNESS OF OUTSIDE AIR]

MARA: No one followed. Lena had her weapon raised at the stairwell behind us. The building held its silence.

MARA: My phone was vibrating.

[BEAT]

MARA: June Bell's number. June was older — she had helped my mother investigate the Palmetto Hotel in the years before the fire. She was one of the last people in Greenville who had known Claire Vance before everything ended.

MARA: Lena looked at the screen. I answered and put it on speaker.

JUNE BELL: Mara, someone is inside my house on Augusta Street.

[SFX: PHONE SHUFFLING — A BRIEF STRUGGLE — SILENCE]

UNKNOWN MAN: Come back to room two seventeen at the old Palmetto Hotel before ten o'clock tonight. Bring the brass token. Do not contact Greenville Police.

[BEAT]

MARA: He didn't know Detective Lena was already listening beside me. I kept my voice level.

MARA: What have you done to June Bell?

UNKNOWN MAN: June Bell is alive. She stays alive if you follow one instruction.

[PAUSE]

UNKNOWN MAN: Bring Eli Mercer alone.

[SFX: PHONE CLICK — THE CALL ENDED]

[SFX: THE REEDY RIVER — DISTANT, PATIENT, BENEATH EVERYTHING]

MARA: I looked at Eli Mercer. The historian who had contacted me eight weeks ago, out of nowhere, with my mother's name on documents I had never found in twenty-nine years of searching. The man whose photograph had been pinned to a wall in a room beneath Falls Park. The man Adrian Cross had given a key to the day before he died.

MARA: The person who had brought me to the bell.

MARA: Eli looked back at me. He said nothing.

MARA: Detective Lena Ortiz was already reaching for her radio.

MARA: And three blocks away, in a windowless room above Main Street, six brass tokens sat in a circle on a folding table — waiting for whoever arrived at ten o'clock.

[END AUDIO DRAMA SCRIPT]

[START AUDIO DRAMA SCRIPT]

NARRATOR: The card was the size of a matchbook. Claire had not left anything to chance.

NARRATOR: Mara read it at Eli's kitchen table in the early morning, on a laptop with the volume low, with Lena across from her and Eli standing at the window with his coffee held in both hands and the particular stillness of someone who understood that the right posture for this moment was to take up as little room as possible.

NARRATOR: The CompactFlash card contained 847 files. Documents, scans, financial records, the original mill land surveys from the early twentieth century, and a digital folder labeled FINANCIAL in Claire's naming convention — her block letters even in filenames, the same handwriting that had been on every note and label and evidence tag for twenty-nine years.

NARRATOR: Mara opened FINANCIAL.

NARRATOR: Forty-one documents. Wire transfer records. Shell company registrations in three states. The financial architecture of a payment that moved from Crowe's primary development account through two shells to a personal account in Norris's wife's name. Twelve separate transfers between 1997 and 2001. The last one in November 2001 — two months after Norris signed the final set of forged documents.

[BEAT]

LENA: She had the money trail.

MARA: She had everything.

LENA: I had the property records. I had the maintenance request discrepancy. I did not have the financial chain. Without it, Norris could have claimed the forgeries were procedural errors — poorly supervised, improperly reviewed, but not directed. The money is the direction.

[PAUSE:2]

NARRATOR: Lena forwarded six specific documents to three addresses: her own, her trusted colleague's, and a prosecutor in the state attorney general's office she had been in quiet contact with for four months.

NARRATOR: The case was complete. It had been incomplete until forty minutes ago. Now it was complete.

[SFX: A laptop closing on a kitchen table at dawn — the specific sound of an investigation reaching the point where the next step is not more evidence but action]

NARRATOR: Mara sat with the closed laptop and thought about her mother sitting in a sub-basement room with a lamp and papers in 1997, building the same case she had just read.

NARRATOR: She thought about what it had cost Claire to build it. What it had cost to come back in 1998 and update it. What it had cost to prepare for the possibility that the room itself would be destroyed and the digital backup would be all that remained.

NARRATOR: Claire had done it anyway.

---

NARRATOR: The meeting with Norris and his attorney was held in a conference room on North Main Street at nine in the morning. Lena, the state attorney general's representative, Norris, and two attorneys in a room with a recording device and a view of a parking lot.

NARRATOR: Norris had made his decision. The truth he was going to tell was the truth he was going to tell — shaped by his attorney's counsel, but truth regardless.

NARRATOR: What Mara had not expected was the name.

[SFX: A recording device placed on a conference table — the small bureaucratic weight of a formal record being made]

LENA: He named the associate. The person who informed him in 2001 that Claire Vance had been found and the situation had been resolved.

MARA: Who.

LENA: A man named Dyer. Gary Dyer. Formerly a Greenville city contractor. Currently employed by Crowe's development company as a site security coordinator. He set the fire in the hotel last night. And there is something else.

[BEAT]

LENA: The description Norris gave of the 2001 meeting — the height, the manner, the quality of movement Norris remembered as deliberate and controlled. It matches the Unknown Man from the Palmetto Hotel.

NARRATOR: Mara was quiet.

NARRATOR: The Unknown Man. The one who had handed her the seventh token in Room two seventeen with the knowledge of someone who understood exactly what it was. The one who had said: someone has been running ahead of you, building the path for your specific arrival.

NARRATOR: Not an ally. A Crowe operative who believed he could use Mara's investigation to observe what Claire's work had survived. He had given her enough to keep moving so he could see every piece before she could act on it. He had believed he could manage her.

LENA: He didn't know about the card. He cleared what he could from the vault before setting the fire. But he never opened the iron token.

MARA: Claire sealed it with enough craft that someone who didn't know to look wouldn't look.

LENA: Yes.

NARRATOR: Mara thought about the iron token she had carried since EP2. Dyer had been in the same vault, had handled six of the seven tokens.

NARRATOR: He had never thought to open the seventh.

NARRATOR: Claire had thought of that, too.

---

NARRATOR: The permit review meeting was held that afternoon in a fourth-floor conference room in the Greenville City Hall annex. Silas Crowe had requested speaking time for an update on his riverfront development project — a request approved two weeks prior.

NARRATOR: He arrived in the expensive jacket. He sat with his attorney. He had the practiced blankness of a man who understood that the room was managed by composure.

NARRATOR: The district attorney's representative entered at two-fifteen.

[SFX: A conference room door opening at the moment everything that has been outside finally comes in — the shift of institutional air in a room about to change]

NARRATOR: What followed was procedural, not dramatic. The DA's representative presented a summary of findings: the forged decommissioning records, the financial records connecting Crowe's development accounts to Norris, the pre-forgery maintenance requests, Norris's formal cooperation statement. The documents were placed in the record.

NARRATOR: Crowe's attorney spoke for four minutes. He used the word "context" three times and "at this stage" twice.

NARRATOR: Crowe said nothing.

NARRATOR: The development permits were frozen pending formal investigation. The property records were referred to the state inspector general.

NARRATOR: Crowe stood when the meeting ended. He buttoned his jacket. He did not look at the DA's representative or at Lena. He looked, for a single moment, at the window — at the Greenville afternoon beyond the glass — with the expression of a man who had spent a long time keeping certain things outside a room, and understood now that the outside had come in.

NARRATOR: Then he left.

[SFX: The conference room settling into ordinary acoustics after the man who was managing it has gone]

NARRATOR: Mara was in Eli's car in the parking structure two blocks away, listening to Lena's account over the phone. When Lena was done, Mara said: what happens now.

LENA: The investigation runs through the AG's office. Dyer is identified as a person of interest in Claire's disappearance — Norris's statement gives us that. The permits are frozen. The fraudulent records are flagged for removal from the city's active files.

MARA: And Claire.

LENA: Claire's case is reclassified. Officially, as of this afternoon, Claire Vance's disappearance from June 18, 1997 is an open homicide investigation. It is on record. It cannot be closed by anyone with a reason to close it.

[PAUSE:3]

MARA: She has been missing for twenty-nine years.

LENA: Yes.

MARA: And now she is officially missing for reasons.

[BEAT]

LENA: The investigation will find what it finds. The evidence is there.

NARRATOR: Mara sat in the parking structure for a long time after the call ended.

NARRATOR: She thought about her mother's voice on the first cassette, in the hidden room beneath Liberty Bridge. I did not choose to go. The seven words that had reorganized everything she understood about her childhood.

NARRATOR: She thought about the second cassette — in the sealed chamber below the Palmetto Hotel, with smoke beginning to gather in the passage. I will not say goodbye. I am not interested in goodbye.

NARRATOR: She thought about the digital copy, on the CompactFlash card, now in three secure locations and in the AG's evidence file.

NARRATOR: Claire's voice. Preserved.

NARRATOR: She could listen to it whenever she wanted.

---

NARRATOR: That evening, Mara sat alone at her own kitchen table — her apartment, the one she had left three weeks ago when she drove to Greenville on the strength of a dying man's request.

NARRATOR: She had returned to her ordinary space in the way people return after something large has happened, and find that ordinary does not quite fit anymore. But fit was not the point.

NARRATOR: She opened the laptop. She found the folder from the CompactFlash card. She found the subfolder labeled CASSETTE 2.

[SFX: A digital audio file opening — a quiet cursor click, then a half-second of silence before the recording begins]

NARRATOR: She pressed play.

[PAUSE:2]

CLAIRE: Mara. If you reached this room, you did the work I could not finish. I want to say that first.

NARRATOR: She listened.

NARRATOR: Not because she needed the recording to tell her anything she did not already know. She had heard it once in a vault that was now ash, with smoke in the passage and twenty-nine years pressing in from every direction at once. This time there was no smoke. No passage. No urgency.

NARRATOR: This time it was a woman in a quiet room, pressing play because she wanted to hear her mother's voice.

NARRATOR: That was enough of a reason.

NARRATOR: The recording ran for fourteen minutes. Claire's voice moved through everything she had found, everything she had prepared, everything she had feared and planned around and tried to account for. Her voice was warm beneath the precision — the warmth of someone who had, for fourteen minutes, decided to stop being careful and simply speak.

NARRATOR: When the recording ended, the apartment was quiet.

NARRATOR: Mara pressed play again.

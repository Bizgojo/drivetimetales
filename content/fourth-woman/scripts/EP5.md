BELLE: She found where three women are being held - and none of them will leave.

NARRATOR: The building was real, it was permitted, and three women inside it would not leave even if someone opened the door.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: Diana called me on day two of the three days she’d asked for — earlier than I expected. The relay number rang at six-fifteen in the morning, and she said four words before anything else: I’m giving you the address.

[SFX: Motel room ambient - early morning, distant highway, HVAC unit]

NARRATOR: I already had it. Not from her - from page thirty-one of her documentation. The permit variance, the property record: a converted riverside facility on the south Reedy bank. I'd cross-referenced the address against county parcel records at the library the previous afternoon. I hadn't told Diana because I was still deciding what to do with it.

[SFX: Phone - slight static on relay line]

DIANA (through phone): The south Reedy property. You need to go today. Not to do anything. To see it. To understand what we're working against, so that what I'm about to ask you to do makes sense.

CLAIRE: What are you about to ask me to do?

DIANA (through phone): Go first. Then we talk.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: The address was a two-story converted warehouse on a stretch of the Reedy's south bank that the development overlay had flagged as Zone Three - mixed-use adaptive reuse, the kind of designation that turns old industrial buildings into event spaces or co-working lofts with enough money and the right permits. The building had been operating as a private facility since February. The permit had a small condition attached - limited occupancy, private use only, no public-facing operations - which, under Greenville's current development variance structure, was exactly the kind of language that meant almost nothing was required to be disclosed.

[SFX: Car - driving, river road ambient, gravel edge, distant water]

NARRATOR: Merritt's own rezoning language had permitted this building. His consortium's variance request had included it as a condition of approval, buried in subsection nine of a forty-seven-page filing. The building existed, legally, within the framework of a vote that hadn't happened yet. That was the detail that had kept me up until three a.m. - the vote had to pass for the full development rights to transfer, but the variance that had permitted this building had already been approved as a precondition. Merritt had used the rezoning process to build his own infrastructure before anyone voted on whether to let him have it.

[SFX: Car slowing - parking on a gravel pull-off near a river road - engine off]

NARRATOR: The building was set back fifty yards from the road, behind a low brick retaining wall and a chain-link fence. Chain-link with privacy screening - green mesh, the kind you see on construction sites. One vehicle in the access lane visible through the gate: a white van. Same type Mara had noted in her field log.

[SFX: Binoculars - light click of adjustment, ambient birdsong]

NARRATOR: I sat in the car with a pair of compact binoculars I'd bought at the outdoor store on the way. Second-floor windows - four of them visible from my position - covered with privacy film. No clear sight line inside. Ground floor: one door on the building's south face, card-reader access, industrial hinges.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: And then: movement. A curtain on the second-floor east window pulled to the side. A woman's face - just for a moment. She looked out at the river road, then back inside. The curtain fell.

[PAUSE:2]

NARRATOR: I put the binoculars down. I didn't move the car for ten minutes. I was running the geometry of what Diana had told me and what I could see in front of me, and trying to reconcile the two against the one thing that kept resisting resolution: those three women, by Diana's account, were not being held by force. They were being held by fear.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: Coercion without locks. That's the hardest kind to break.

[SFX: Car door - opening quietly - footsteps on gravel, very deliberate]

NARRATOR: I walked toward the fence. Not all the way - I stopped at the property line, which was marked by a low concrete boundary marker. I stood there in plain sight for about thirty seconds, facing the building. If there were cameras, I was on them. I wanted to be - not as a provocation, but as a statement. I wanted whoever was inside to know that someone was on the road.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: The east-window curtain moved again. The same woman - or a different one, I couldn't be certain at this distance. She was looking directly at me.

[SFX: Wind off the river - slow, steady]

NARRATOR: I held up one hand. Not a wave. Just: I see you. I'm here.

[PAUSE:2]

NARRATOR: The curtain closed.

[SFX: Footsteps back to the car - gravel - car door]

NARRATOR: I called Diana on the relay.

[SFX: Phone dialing - relay connecting]

CLAIRE: I'm at the property. I saw someone in the second-floor window.

DIANA (through phone): East window?

CLAIRE: Yes.

DIANA (through phone): That's Renata's room. She watches the road.

[BEAT]

CLAIRE: Diana. I need to understand something. You said they won't leave. What does that mean, exactly? If someone opened that gate and walked in-

DIANA (through phone): I tried. Two weeks ago. I sent a contact - someone completely clean, no connection to me or this situation. She went to the building under the cover of being lost, needing directions. She managed two minutes at the door before the Vantage employee turned her away. In those two minutes, she briefly saw Mara Fields in the interior. She passed a note. Paper, in her palm, hand-to-hand in the doorway before the door closed.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through phone): The note said: If there's a way out, will you take it?

CLAIRE: What did Mara say?

DIANA (through phone): Nothing. She passed a note back. Three words: Not yet safe.

[PAUSE:1]

CLAIRE: Not safe for who?

DIANA (through phone): Her sister's family lives in Taylors - about eight miles north of Greenville. Three weeks before Mara went into that building, her sister's husband lost his trucking contract. Largest single contract his business had. The firm that cancelled it had no prior history with the family. No explanation given. Mara understood exactly what it meant.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through phone): Renata's daughter had a graduate fellowship rescinded - a foundation that had never rescinded a fellowship in its history sent a form letter saying her application was no longer competitive. Diane Cho's brother had two of his freelance contracts - he's a sound engineer - cancelled in the same week. Small demonstrations. Clear enough to communicate: we know your family's vulnerabilities, and we will use them.

CLAIRE: And what stops Merritt from using those vulnerabilities the moment the women are free?

DIANA (through phone): Nothing, if they're simply released without the full structure being dismantled. Which is why the vote has to be stopped with evidence - not with three women walking out of a building. If the evidence gets out, if Merritt and Vantage are publicly implicated before the vote, then retaliating against the women's families becomes one more visible crime in a pile they're already trying to manage.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through phone): The leverage has to break the hold before we open the gate. That's the sequence. That's why I've been building this for ninety days.

CLAIRE: Four days left, Diana.

DIANA (through phone): I know.

[SFX: Silence on the line - wind off the river in the background]

CLAIRE: Ellen Marsh's office called back. Through the relay I gave them. She'll meet me tomorrow - secure location, federal building adjacent conference space, a contact of hers facilitates. She's aware enough of the broader inquiry context that she'll know what she's looking at.

DIANA (through phone): Good. Take the financial documentation. Keep Mara's field notes separate - those are your independent corroboration, and I want them disclosed in a different channel at a different moment.

CLAIRE: Why?

DIANA (through phone): Because two independent streams of evidence are harder to suppress than one. If something goes wrong with the federal meeting, the field notes are still intact. If something goes wrong with the field notes, the financial documentation is still with Marsh. They don't both fail at the same time.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I sat in the car on the river road and looked at the warehouse building through the windshield. A building that was legally permitted to exist. A building that held three women who wouldn't leave, not because the door was locked, but because the price of leaving without the right conditions had already been demonstrated to them in the language they understood most clearly: the people they loved, made smaller and more vulnerable, one targeted loss at a time.

[SFX: River ambient - slow, constant water sound]

NARRATOR: I thought about what Diana had said: ninety days, watching. Planning. Finding the right journalist, the right chain of evidence, the right sequence. Patient in a way I hadn't been asked to be patient before. I was on day three.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I drove back toward the east side. I had documentation to organize. A federal meeting to prepare for. Three days - now four, now three and counting - before a vote that would convert a billion dollars in development rights and leave three women's silence as the only collateral that had ever mattered.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: Before I got on the highway, I pulled over once more. I turned and looked back at the warehouse building - just visible from the ramp, the green-mesh privacy fence, the privacy-filmed windows.

[PAUSE:1]

NARRATOR: The east window. The curtain moved.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I don't know if she was still watching. I'd like to think she was.

[PAUSE:2]

NARRATOR: I made one more call before I got on the highway. Not to Diana. Not to the relay. I called my editor at WGVL.

CLAIRE: Mark. It's Claire. I need you to do something for me and I need you to not ask questions yet.

EDITOR (through phone): Claire - where have you been? Reyes called the station this morning asking-

CLAIRE: I know. Don't tell her anything. Don't confirm or deny where I am. And pull up everything we have on Dale Merritt - every council story, every vote, every public statement for the last four years. Don't publish anything. Just have it ready.

EDITOR (through phone): Claire-

CLAIRE: Four days, Mark. I need four days. And then I'm going to bring you a story that'll run national.

[BEAT]

[SFX: Phone call ending]

NARRATOR: He said okay. He didn't ask. That's why I've worked for him for twelve years.

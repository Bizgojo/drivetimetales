BELLE: Diana has been silent for eighteen hours — and what she left behind for Claire might be the only thing that saves them both.

NARRATOR: Diana Voss went silent on a Tuesday morning, one day before the rezoning vote. And I didn't know it right away, which is the part I've thought about most since then — how many of those eighteen hours I spent doing other things, trusting that the relay would ring, never once considering that it wouldn't.

[SFX: Federal building adjacent conference room — quiet, institutional, a distant HVAC tone]

NARRATOR: The meeting with Ellen Marsh had gone better than I'd hoped. She was exactly what Diana's description had prepared me for — methodical, careful, someone who listened to a source the way a surgeon listens to a heartbeat: without interrupting, without leading, without giving away what she was hearing until she'd heard all of it. We spent ninety minutes in a nondescript conference room. I walked her through the financial documentation in sequence: Merritt's LLC structure, the pre-vote permit variance, the Vantage Group contract, the second countersigning official's name on page thirty-one.

[SFX: Paper on a conference table — documents being reviewed]

NARRATOR: She asked four questions. All four were the right questions. When I finished, she was quiet for a moment, then she said: we have a file on Merritt. Separate matter — a federal contracts inquiry from two years ago that stalled on jurisdictional grounds. What you've brought me changes the jurisdictional picture.

[BEAT]

MARSHALL (through memory/narration): I can't tell you the timing of what happens next. What I can tell you is that I will not take action that endangers those three women or compromises their safety. I need twenty-four hours to brief my team. After that — you and I are in contact daily until this resolves.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: She gave me a secure contact number. Not a relay — a dedicated encrypted line run through her office. I left the building from the parking structure exit, a different route than I'd arrived, in a car I'd swapped from the morning's rental.

[SFX: Parking garage — footsteps on concrete, car door, engine starting]

NARRATOR: Tuesday morning. The relay was quiet. I assumed Diana was moving — she'd told me she changed locations on unpredictable cycles, that I might not hear from her for twelve to fourteen hours at a stretch. I worked. I organized my notes. I drafted the arc of the story the way I would if I were building a broadcast segment — not to file it, just to understand what I had and what was still missing.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: By noon: still quiet. I ate lunch. I drove a slow route to a different motel on the east side. I called my editor. I did not call the relay — Diana had been explicit that I should not initiate contact through it without cause, because initiating created a pattern that could be detected.

[SFX: Motel room — afternoon light, quiet, distant road]

NARRATOR: By six p.m., eighteen hours since her last call: I called.

[SFX: Phone dialing — relay tone — ringing — no answer — relay voicemail tone]

NARRATOR: Relay voicemail. No Diana. I left nothing. I called again at six-fifteen. Again at seven. Each time: relay voicemail. Each time, I hung up without speaking.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: At seven-forty-five, a text came through the relay number. Not a voice message — a text. Four words: Check the second locker.

[PAUSE:2]

NARRATOR: I hadn't known there was a second locker.

[SFX: Car — night driving — Greenville streets, heading toward the old train terminal]

NARRATOR: I'd been to Locker 214 once. It had been prepared for me — the headphones, the note. Diana had used that locker as the first link in the chain that connected the suitcase to her. If there was a second locker, she'd had it ready the entire time. Which meant she'd built a failsafe before the first contact. Before she knew how this would unfold. Before she knew if I was the right journalist.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: That's ninety days of planning. That's someone who never assumed any part of this would work the way she hoped.

[SFX: Old train terminal — night — the concourse quieter, antique seller closed, footsteps echoing more sharply]

NARRATOR: The terminal's emergency lighting was on — amber strips along the floor, the main overheads dark. The locker bank on the north wall. I passed 214. I kept walking. There was a second locker bank I'd never gone past, around the corner at the north end, behind where the old ticketing windows had been. Less visible. Not part of the main concourse traffic pattern.

[SFX: Footsteps — corner turn — different tile underfoot, slightly older sound]

NARRATOR: A small section of lockers, older than the main bank — brass more tarnished, numbers in a different stamping style. I had the original key fob — the one from the burner phone in the suitcase, the one that had opened 214. I held it up to each locker until one of them went green.

[SFX: Key fob beep — LED click — different locker, slightly smaller, a lower creak]

NARRATOR: Locker 47. Inside: a hard drive. Sealed in a waterproof case identical to the one that had held the suitcase contents. And an index card — handwritten, Diana's careful print — with two lines:

[BEAT]

CLAIRE: Financial records, full transaction chain. Communications — text and email, extracted. Full list of compromised officials, fourteen names.

[PAUSE:1]

CLAIRE: If you're reading this, I'm either safe and couldn't reach you in time, or I'm not safe. Either way, this is everything. Get it to Marsh. Then go live.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I stood in the amber light of the old terminal at eight-twelve p.m. on a Tuesday, holding a hard drive that represented everything Diana Voss had spent ninety days building, apparently for this precise contingency. And I tried to decide whether the phrase "if I'm not safe" meant what I thought it meant.

[SFX: Terminal ambient — very quiet — distant ventilation, the sound of the building at night]

NARRATOR: I pocketed the hard drive. I turned for the exit.

[SFX: Footsteps — stopped — other footsteps — two, three other sets — from the main concourse]

NARRATOR: And I heard them. From the concourse behind me — two sets of footsteps, deliberate, not casual. Not the antique seller. Not a late traveler. Moving with the specific pace of people who are covering ground toward a destination rather than wandering.

[SFX: Footsteps — closer — around the corner from the main bank]

NARRATOR: Two men. Business casual, nothing conspicuous. The one in front was holding a phone with the screen toward me — my photo on it. He looked at the phone, looked at me.

MAN: Ms. Bennett. We just need to talk.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I have walked into a lot of situations over twelve years on a crime beat. I have learned to read a room — or, in this case, a terminal — very quickly. The man in front was relaxed. The one behind him was not. The one behind him was doing the thing that security professionals do when they are trying not to appear to be security professionals: standing too still in a way that required active effort.

[BEAT]

MAN: We can make this easy. You haven't filed anything yet — we know that. Whatever you've been given, whatever you think you have — there's a conversation to be had before this goes any further.

[PAUSE:1]

NARRATOR: The hard drive was in my left jacket pocket. My phone was in my right hand. The south exit was twenty feet behind me, around the second corner — the same door I'd used the first night, the one Diana had directed me to.

[BEAT]

MAN: Ms. Bennett.

NARRATOR: I said the only thing that seemed accurate.

CLAIRE: There's nothing to talk about. The documentation is already with a federal prosecutor. Whatever you were sent here to accomplish is already past its window.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: The man in front was good. He kept his face neutral. But the one behind him shifted his weight, just slightly, and I saw the calculation run across his expression before he controlled it: if that's true, stopping her now doesn't fix anything. If that's not true, stopping her now might be everything.

[BEAT]

MAN: Then you won't mind sitting down for a conversation.

NARRATOR: I looked at him for two seconds.

CLAIRE: I mind very much.

[SFX: Footsteps — fast now — Claire moving toward the south corner]

NARRATOR: I didn't run. Running is conspicuous and it commits you to a direction. I walked fast, with purpose — the way you walk through an airport when your gate is closing. I went around the second corner, through the secondary concourse, hit the south exit bar at full extension.

[SFX: Exit bar — metal crash — door flying open — cool night air — city ambient rushing in]

NARRATOR: I was on West Washington Street in under ten seconds. I turned west instead of east — the opposite of the direction Diana had sent me on the first night. I was three blocks away before I heard the terminal door open behind me. I didn't look back.

[SFX: Night street — walking fast — distant traffic, faint footsteps behind, then gone]

NARRATOR: I made one call as I walked.

[SFX: Phone dialing — ringing twice]

MARSHAL (through phone): Marsh.

CLAIRE: It's Bennett. I need the secure line active tonight. I'm coming in with the hard drive. And I need you to know: Vantage Group just sent two people to a train terminal in downtown Greenville to intercept me. They know I've been meeting with you. I don't know how they know — but the window is closing faster than either of us thought.

[BEAT]

MARSHAL (through phone): Can you reach the building tonight?

CLAIRE: Tell me what I'm walking into if I do.

MARSHAL (through phone): My team. Two FBI agents who've been briefed. Full custody of the drive. After tonight, the matter is in federal hands and your legal exposure is covered by source protection.

CLAIRE: And the three women on the south Reedy?

MARSHAL (through phone): Tomorrow. First light. I need tonight to do this right.

[PAUSE:1]

NARRATOR: The vote was tomorrow afternoon. The women had been in that building for weeks. Diana had been dark for eighteen hours, and I didn't know where she was or if she was safe. And I was on a street in downtown Greenville holding a hard drive that represented ninety days of work by a woman I'd spoken to exactly four times.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I thought about what Diana had said in the terminal, on the first night, after I'd put the headphones on: they told everyone we disappeared. And the thing she hadn't said, the thing that sat underneath every word she'd spoken since: we didn't disappear because we chose not to. We disappeared because we had no one to choose for us.

[PAUSE:2]

NARRATOR: I told Ellen Marsh where to send her people to meet me. Then I put the phone in my pocket, and I walked.

[START AUDIO DRAMA SCRIPT]

BELLE: Someone Claire trusted has been reporting her every move to the man she's investigating — and now she's six days from a vote she can't stop if they find her first.

NARRATOR: The night after I found Mara's field notes, someone tampered with my car.

[SFX: Parking garage — early morning, concrete echo, distant mechanical ventilation]

NARRATOR: Friday morning, seven a.m. My car in the covered garage half a block from my apartment. I found it because I looked — not because anything appeared obviously wrong. After the text the day before — Stop digging, Bennett — I'd made a decision the previous evening: I would treat every environment as potentially compromised until I understood who had sent it. Which meant checking my car the way the police scanner had once taught me to check things: methodically, from the outside in.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: The brake line hadn't been cut. It was something subtler and more frightening — a small, precise interference with the fluid reservoir cap, creating the conditions for a slow bleed under sustained pressure. At highway speed, eventually. Not immediate. Deniable. The kind of thing that looks like deferred maintenance in the accident report.

[SFX: Car hood lowering — quiet in the garage — Claire's breath audible for a moment]

NARRATOR: I stood in that garage for three minutes. Not panicking. Thinking. Someone had done this overnight. Someone who knew where I parked. Someone with enough knowledge and access to make it look like nothing.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I took photos. I did not call Detective Reyes.

[SFX: Phone camera shutter — two clicks]

NARRATOR: That omission tells you where I'd landed by that morning. Reyes had said eight words and hung up. The text had arrived fourteen seconds later from someone monitoring either her line or mine. Diana had warned me: three names in the municipal and law enforcement structure were on Vantage's contact list. I didn't know Reyes was one of them. I didn't know she wasn't.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I couldn't afford to make that mistake. Not with six days left before the rezoning vote. Not with forty-three pages of documentation in a kraft envelope in my apartment. I left my car in the garage. I walked to a rental agency four blocks away, paid cash — an inconvenience I'd prepared for the night before — and took a compact sedan in a color I would not have chosen under any other circumstances.

[SFX: Rental agency — door bell chime, brief transaction, keys in hand]

NARRATOR: I went off-grid. That phrase sounds dramatic. What it actually means is: I used the burner I'd bought at a convenience store, I sent nothing from my personal devices, I communicated with my editor through a message drop we'd agreed on years ago for source protection, and I reached Diana through the relay number the headphones had connected to.

[SFX: New phone — plastic crinkle of a prepaid blister pack, then dial tones]

DIANA (through phone): You found something.

CLAIRE: The field notes are real. I've got photos of all fourteen pages — GPS coordinates, construction markers, plate numbers on vehicles that shouldn't have been there. I also picked up the folder. The rezoning financial structure is documented. And my car was tampered with last night.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through phone): Brake?

CLAIRE: Fluid reservoir. Slow bleed. Looked like maintenance failure if the accident report didn't ask the right questions.

DIANA (through phone): That's Vantage's second-tier response. They're not certain what you have yet, which means they're not at the removal stage. But they're evaluating.

CLAIRE: What's the removal stage?

[PAUSE:1]

DIANA (through phone): I need you to stay off your regular routes. Don't go to the station. Don't go home unless it's after eleven p.m. and you've varied your approach twice.

CLAIRE: That's not an answer to the question.

DIANA (through phone): No. It's the answer to the question that matters right now.

[BEAT]

CLAIRE: Diana. You told me three names in the municipal and law enforcement structure are on Vantage's contact list. I made a call to one of my sources yesterday — a detective. Fourteen seconds after I hung up, an anonymous text came through telling me to stop. I need to know if that detective is one of the three names.

[LONG PAUSE]

DIANA (through phone): Tell me who you called.

CLAIRE: Detective Reyes. She handled the Osei case from the beginning.

DIANA (through phone): Not Reyes directly. But her lieutenant is. Lieutenant Harmon. He gets copies of significant case calls — standard protocol, which means Reyes doesn't know she's part of a monitoring chain. The text didn't come from her. It came from upstream.

[BEAT]

CLAIRE: So she's not compromised — she's being used.

DIANA (through phone): Carefully. She's a good detective. They needed her working the case without actually solving it. Harmon manages the flow of what she finds and what gets escalated. She thinks she's running an investigation. She's running a controlled one.

[PAUSE:1]

NARRATOR: I thought about Reyes. Four full seconds when she'd looked at me at the river bank before letting me see the suitcase. The calculation in that look. I'd read it as: is this journalist useful. Maybe it was: I don't know who's listening to my phone. Maybe she was asking for help in the only language she could use.

[SFX: Car interior — Claire parked on a quiet street, engine off, traffic distant]

CLAIRE: Six days to the vote. What do we do?

DIANA (through phone): Two things need to happen simultaneously. The financial records need to reach a federal jurisdiction — someone whose chain of command is completely outside Harmon's reach. And the three women need to be freed before the vote, because if the vote passes and Merritt has what he wants, there's no leverage left to compel their release.

CLAIRE: If I take the documentation to the FBI—

DIANA (through phone): You can't walk into a federal building without Vantage knowing within the hour. Not from your current profile. They have eyes on federal building approaches for exactly this scenario.

CLAIRE: Then how?

DIANA (through phone): There's a federal prosecutor in the Western District — Ellen Marsh. She's been running a parallel inquiry into municipal development corruption — entirely separate matter, nothing to do with Merritt directly, but overlapping jurisdiction. She doesn't report through any structure Harmon can see. I have a contact address for her. But I can't make the approach myself — I'm officially missing. Any documented contact from me gets analyzed and potentially intercepted before it reaches her desk.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through phone): It has to be you. In person. No paper trail until you're in the room.

CLAIRE: And the women?

DIANA (through phone): That has to come second. I know how that sounds.

CLAIRE: It sounds like three people are spending another six days in a building on the south Reedy bank waiting for us to manage our process correctly.

DIANA (through phone): It sounds like that because that's what it is. I've had ninety days to be angry about it. You'll have six.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I didn't respond for a moment. Diana was right — tactically, completely, in every way that mattered. And it was still a sentence that landed like something physical.

[SFX: Wind outside the car — a passing truck on a nearby street]

CLAIRE: I need to know where they are. The property address. I want to see it myself.

DIANA (through phone): Not yet.

CLAIRE: Diana—

DIANA (through phone): Not yet. I need you functional, not reactive. You've just had your car tampered with. Going to that property today, in your current state, would be the fastest way to tell Vantage exactly how much you know and where you're standing.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through phone): Three days. I need three days to prepare something. And I need you to get to Ellen Marsh in that window. Once she's in possession of the financial documentation, the clock changes for us.

CLAIRE: Three days.

DIANA (through phone): I'll contact you through the relay. Change your pickup location every day — I'll reach you wherever you are. Don't use the same café twice.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through phone): Claire. How are you doing with all of this?

[PAUSE:1]

CLAIRE: I'm doing fine.

DIANA (through phone): That's the answer someone gives when they're not doing fine but they're committed to continuing anyway. I respect it. Just — eat something today. It helps.

[BEAT]

[SFX: Phone call ending]

NARRATOR: I ate something. She was right about that, too.

[SFX: Short transition — ambient time passing, a brief stretch of road noise]

NARRATOR: That afternoon, I drove to a public library branch on the east side — no cameras covering my parking approach, an older building with a public entrance on two streets. I used a reading room, a physical reference copy of Western District court filings, and the library's landline to leave a message for Ellen Marsh through her public office number. I identified myself as a journalist with source-protected documentation relevant to a municipal development corruption inquiry. I said I'd be in touch within forty-eight hours to arrange a meeting.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: On the way back to the car, I passed a pay phone — there are still three in Greenville, outside holdouts — and I stopped. I stood looking at it for a moment.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: Then I kept walking.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: Six days. Diana off-grid, reachable only through a relay I couldn't control. Three women in a riverside building I hadn't seen yet. A federal prosecutor who didn't know my face. And somewhere upstream in the chain of command at Greenville PD, a lieutenant named Harmon who was getting copies of every significant call made by the detective assigned to a case I was in the middle of.

[PAUSE:2]

NARRATOR: The vote was six days away. Which meant everything I needed to do had to happen in five. Because on the sixth day, if the vote passed, Merritt had what he wanted, and a billion-dollar development deal becomes the wall between three women and any public interest in what happened to them.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I checked into a motel on the east side. Cash. Fake name — the night clerk didn't look at the registration card. I sat on the bed with the kraft envelope and went through every page of Diana's documentation for the third time.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I was looking for the thing I'd missed. The detail that, when I found it, would tell me where the whole structure was weakest.

[PAUSE:1]

NARRATOR: I found it on page thirty-one.

[PAUSE:2]

NARRATOR: A permit variance — the one authorizing the private event facility on the south Reedy bank. Signed by Merritt. Countersigned by a second municipal official. The second official's name: someone I hadn't expected. Someone whose job had nothing to do with land use.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: Someone whose job had everything to do with who got to vote on the rezoning in ten days.

BELLE: Every missing woman left behind a piece of evidence — and Claire has to find it before the vote erases everything.

NARRATOR: Mara Fields had hidden a second copy of her field notes before Vantage took her — and Diana Voss had just told me exactly where.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I didn't sleep. That's not a figure of speech — I sat at my kitchen table from two in the morning until six, with nothing written down and nothing plugged in, working through what Diana Voss had told me in the dark of the old train terminal. I did it the way my first editor taught me twenty years ago: in my head, in order, start to finish, until the shape of the story was solid enough to trust.

[SFX: Kitchen ambient — early morning, refrigerator hum, distant birdsong beginning]

NARRATOR: What I had: a forensic accountant who had deliberately vanished to stay ahead of a private security firm working for a city councilman. Three women held, not kidnapped in any traditional sense — coerced and contained. A billion-dollar rezoning vote in ten days. And a folder waiting for me at a coffee shop that I wasn't certain was safe to be seen picking up.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: What I needed: the field notes. If Diana was right about the location, Mara Fields had hidden a second copy of her trail documentation before Vantage got to her. Documentation of unauthorized land-clearing inside protected park land. That was the piece of evidence I could get to independently, without anyone's cooperation — without Diana, without the three women, without any source I had to protect.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I was in Falls Park by seven-fifteen.

[SFX: Falls Park ambient — morning birdsong, water over rocks in the distance, light breeze through trees, footsteps on a packed-earth trail]

NARRATOR: Falls Park on the Reedy is one of the things Greenville gets genuinely right. Thirty-two acres of river corridor running through the center of downtown — the waterfall, the suspension bridge, the walking trails threading through old hardwood and birch along the Reedy's banks. On a Thursday morning in May, it was quiet. A few early joggers. A man with a dog. Nobody who looked like they were watching me.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: The suspension bridge at the park's lower level — the old pedestrian span — was the landmark. Diana had said three-tenths of a mile past it on the overlook trail, east side.

[SFX: Suspension bridge underfoot — slight sway, metal cable creak, water rushing below]

NARRATOR: Three-tenths is about six minutes at a deliberate walk. I counted. The overlook trail climbs east from the bridge, working up through the ridge above the Reedy's east bank. Birch trees come in about halfway up — the bark going from river-bottom gray to that characteristic white as the trail levels out.

[SFX: Footsteps transitioning — packed earth to slightly more uneven, leaf-covered ground]

NARRATOR: Three birches together, close enough that their root systems were visibly tangled. The middle one had a faded orange blaze mark at eye level — an old trail marking, the paint mostly gone, just the ghost of it left in the bark. Survey stake on the east side of the trail: gray fiberglass rod, cap flagging faded to almost nothing, pushed down close to ground level. Easy to miss if you weren't looking.

[SFX: Rustling — careful movement through leaf litter near the base of a tree]

NARRATOR: Two feet off the trail. I went down on one knee and pressed both hands into the ground until I felt it — a hard edge under the leaf layer. A waterproof capsule about the size of a thermos, sealed with a screw-top. The kind you can buy at any outdoor store for ten dollars.

[SFX: Capsule being unsealed — slight vacuum pop, then the dry rustle of folded paper]

NARRATOR: Inside: fourteen pages of handwritten notes, folded and sealed in a second layer of plastic. Mara Fields' handwriting — I recognized it from her trail log in the suitcase, the careful precise hand that Diana had described. Dated entries. GPS coordinates. Descriptions.

[SFX: Paper unfolding — careful, deliberate]

CLAIRE: Unauthorized soil preparation, GPS 34.8526° N, 82.4015° W. Perimeter staking consistent with foundation prep. No environmental impact assessment on file for this parcel.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: The coordinates put it at the south end of Falls Park, in the parcel adjacent to the park boundary — the land that appeared on Renata Osei's development overlay map as part of the proposed Reedy River corridor rezoning. The land that, according to the overlay, was not scheduled to be developed until after a successful council vote. The land that someone had already been prepping for construction eight weeks before that vote was due.

[SFX: Wind through birch trees above — a slow, light movement]

CLAIRE: Survey stakes removed and replaced with construction-grade markers, GPS 34.8501° N, 82.4027° W, consistent with a building footprint. Markers are not park service property. Not park service placement.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: Page seven. Mara had noted the date, the time, and the names of two vehicles — license plates recorded — present at the survey site on the morning she'd visited. A white work van and a black SUV. I photographed every page.

[SFX: Phone camera shutter — multiple clicks, methodical]

NARRATOR: I resealed the capsule and put it back exactly where I'd found it. I smoothed the leaves. I stood up, brushed off my knees, and walked back down the trail to the suspension bridge at a pace that said: morning hike, nothing to see here.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: The folder from Diana was waiting at the coffee shop — I stopped there on the way back to my car. Left under the counter at the pick-up window in a plain kraft envelope with my first name on it. No last name. The barista said a woman had dropped it off before they opened. She described someone I didn't recognize — and I was reasonably sure Diana hadn't been there herself. Someone working with her, or a courier she'd arranged.

[SFX: Coffee shop ambient — low morning murmur, espresso machine, door chime]

NARRATOR: I took the envelope to my car. Parked on a side street off North Main, engine off, checked my mirrors twice before I opened it.

[SFX: Envelope opening — kraft paper, the crisp sound of folded documents]

NARRATOR: Forty-three pages. Hard copies. No electronic trace. Diana was thorough. The financial structure of the Reedy River rezoning — Merritt's name appearing through three layers of LLC filings connected to the primary development consortium. Pre-approval construction permits issued before the formal rezoning vote, signed by a municipal official whose name I recognized. Vantage Group's contract with Merritt's consortium — redacted in places, but enough to establish the relationship. And a property record: a converted riverside facility on the south Reedy bank, permitted as a private event space in February, variance attached to Merritt's rezoning package as a condition of approval.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: The address. I didn't go there. Not yet. Not without understanding what I was walking into.

[SFX: Car interior — quiet — distant street noise]

NARRATOR: I called Detective Reyes at eleven forty-three a.m. Standard call — I told her I'd been thinking about the Liberty Bridge suitcase, wanted to follow up on the development map found inside, specifically the Revision 7 overlay designation. Was there anything she could tell me about the Reedy River rezoning vote and any connection to the missing persons cases?

CLAIRE: Detective — I've been doing some background on the Reedy River rezoning. There's a vote coming up in ten days. I'm seeing some interesting LLC connections to Councilman Merritt. Is any of that on your radar in relation to the three cases?

[BEAT]

[SFX: Phone line — slight compression sound]

DETECTIVE REYES (through phone): We don't comment on active investigative avenues, Claire. You know that.

CLAIRE: Of course. I just wanted to flag it in case it was useful.

DETECTIVE REYES (through phone): I'll make a note.

[SFX: Phone call ending — a soft click]

NARRATOR: Twelve-second call. Rote, professional, unremarkable. And then, as I was pulling out of the parking spot, my phone buzzed. A text from an unknown number — not Diana's relay, a different format entirely.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: Three words: Stop digging, Bennett.

[SFX: Car engine — the street noise outside — Claire sitting still for a moment]

NARRATOR: I drove home. I didn't speed. I didn't take a longer route. I acted exactly like someone who'd gotten an unimportant text and was continuing her day. I used every professional instinct I'd built over twelve years to keep my face neutral and my hands steady on the wheel.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: Inside: I was running a single, quiet, very cold calculation. Detective Reyes had picked up, said eight words, and hung up. The text had arrived fourteen seconds later. From a number that wasn't hers — but from someone who knew I'd made that call. Someone who was either listening to Reyes or listening to me.

[PAUSE:2]

NARRATOR: Diana had said three names in the municipal and law enforcement structure were on Vantage's contact list. She hadn't told me which three. I'd assumed that was caution about protecting the investigation. Now I was beginning to wonder if it was caution about protecting me.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I had forty-three pages of financial documentation. I had fourteen pages of Mara Fields' field notes on my phone. I had a converted riverside property address and nine days until the rezoning vote.

[PAUSE:1]

NARRATOR: And someone had just told me to stop. Which meant, for the first time since I photographed a number off a sealed evidence bag at six in the morning, I was close enough to matter.

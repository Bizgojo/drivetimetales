### 1. CLAIRE BENNETT / NARRATOR

**1 · Name & Role**  
Claire Bennett. Crime reporter, WGVL News Radio, Greenville SC. Protagonist, first-person narrator, investigative engine. Every NARRATOR block is her interior voice. She is the series.

**2 · Age & Gender**  
Approximately 42. Female.  
_(She has worked crime reporting for 12 years; her first editor taught her 20 years ago — career start circa age 22, placing her in the low-to-mid 40s.)_

**3 · Vocal Quality / Mood**  
Warm-controlled. Broadcast-trained clarity without broadcast stiffness. The voice of someone who has read victim impact statements on-air without breaking — not cold, but forged. Clipped precision in direct dialogue; slightly fuller and more reflective in narration. Clear mid-range, no fry, no breathiness. The warmth is underneath, not on top.  
**ElevenLabs descriptors:** warm, controlled, measured, clear, journalist-cadence, composed undertow

**4 · Accent / Regional**  
Neutral American with light Upstate South Carolina softening — twelve years in Greenville. Not drawl, not broad Southern; a slight rounding of vowels, a small slowing of consonants in reflective passages. Hard to place but not mid-Atlantic.

**5 · Emotional Register Across the Arc**  
- **EP1-PROMO:** Pure professional control. "I'm not someone who hesitates."  
- **EP1-LIBRARY:** First crack: admits she was afraid to dial the phone. Still composed.  
- **EP2:** Fear managed by training — hands shake in the coffee shop but voice did not. Active, rapid, reactive.  
- **EP3:** Cold intelligence working a problem. Calculated. Slightly harder.  
- **EP4:** Under direct threat, adapts quickly. Some suppressed urgency; the line "I'm doing fine" is the tell.  
- **EP5:** Philosophical register enters. The curtain and the raised hand. She is being changed by the women she hasn't met yet.  
- **EP6:** High-stakes sprint. Urgency at its peak. Physical threat in the terminal.  
- **EP7:** Quiet resolution. The broadcast reads with authority. On the bridge: still, awed, stepping back deliberately. Ends in earned peace.  
**Arc shift:** Controlled → threatened → humanized → resolved. The warmth that was buried in EP1 is above the surface in EP7.

### 2. DIANA VOSS

**1 · Name & Role**  
Diana Voss. Forensic accountant. The "fourth woman" — she staged her own disappearance to stay ahead of Vantage Group and build the evidentiary chain that will expose them. Deuteragonist, mastermind, architect of the entire plot. Heard primarily through headphones (EP1) and phone relay (EP2, EP4, EP5); in person only in EP7.

**2 · Age & Gender**  
43. Female.  
_(Stated explicitly in EP7: "Diana Voss was forty-three years old.")_

**3 · Vocal Quality / Mood**  
Clipped authority with clinical precision. Short declarative sentences, no wasted syllables. Does not explain herself unless strategically necessary. Controlled even under extreme pressure. Not cold — she shows brief humanity twice (the EP4 "eat something" and the EP7 exhausted relief) — but the default is tactical command. Slightly higher formality register than Claire. The voice of someone who has rehearsed every sentence.  
**ElevenLabs descriptors:** authoritative, clipped, precise, controlled, measured, clinical undertone, strategic cadence

**4 · Accent / Regional**  
Neutral American — professional mobility, no regional anchor. Financial professional, possibly credentialed up north or Midwest; no Southern softening. Broadcast-clean but not broadcast-warm.

**5 · Emotional Register Across the Arc**  
- **EP1-PROMO / EP1-LIBRARY:** Three cryptic lines in the dark. Theatrical in their brevity. Completely controlled.  
- **EP2:** Active tactical briefing — detailed, fast, commanding. Shows urgency through pace, not pitch.  
- **EP4:** Tactical through threat discussion. One moment of warmth ("eat something today. It helps.") — a tell of the person beneath the operator.  
- **EP5:** Stakes acknowledged as personal for the first time. The three women's families become real. "I've had ninety days to be angry about it."  
- **EP6:** Absent (silent, on the run).  
- **EP7:** Exhausted, human, present. Hair pulled back, jacket worn for days, "completely, entirely present." For the first time: relief, not tactics. "It worked." The arc closes here.  
**Arc shift:** Tactical cryptic → active command → briefly human → exhausted relief. Emotion enters late and lands hard precisely because it's so rare.

## CASTING DIRECTOR NOTES

**The EP7 Bridge Scene** is the highest-complexity vocal moment in the series. Five female voices speak in sequence or overlap: CLAIRE, DIANA, RENATA, DIANE CHO. (BELLE does not appear within scenes.) Any listener who cannot immediately identify which woman is speaking has lost the emotional payoff of the entire series. Cast this scene first. Build all other casting decisions from it.

**Diana/Diane Name Collision:** Strongly recommend the voice director address this in the performance brief. The names are differentiated in text; they are not differentiated in sound. The solution is casting: these two characters must be immediately distinct at the texture and pitch level. Consider: Diana Voss as a slightly lower, more controlled contralto; Diane Cho as a lighter, higher, quieter register.

**Phone/Relay Filter:** Diana speaks through headphones (EP1) and phone relay (EP2, EP4, EP5) for most of the series. The audio processing of these filter effects will assist differentiation in those episodes. EP7 removes all filters — Diana is in-person for the first time. The voice must hold up as clearly distinct from Claire without any technical assist.

**MARA FIELDS — NOTE:** Mara Fields is a central named character referenced in every episode, but she carries **no speaker tag** in any script. Her words are always reported or narrated by Claire. If the production team wishes to add Mara's voice for EP7 or an extended scene, she would require a full casting profile. As written, she requires no voice cast.

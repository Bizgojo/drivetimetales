[START AUDIO DRAMA SCRIPT]

BELLE: Three women disappeared in Greenville — and the fourth one had been hiding on purpose, waiting for the right journalist to find her.

NARRATOR: In the dark, in the old train terminal, a woman's voice said my name — and the twelve years of beat reporting that had taught me to stay calm in crisis did absolutely nothing for me.

[SFX: Train terminal — complete darkness — distant city ambient bleeding through old masonry, very faint]

NARRATOR: I stood at Locker 214 with borrowed headphones over my ears and didn't move. Not from strategy. From simple, direct, animal stillness — the kind that happens before the brain catches up.

[PAUSE:2]

DIANA (through headphones): Are you still there?

[BEAT]

CLAIRE: Yes. I'm here.

DIANA (through headphones): Good. Don't speak too loudly. There are microphones on that level that aren't mine.

[BEAT]

CLAIRE: Whose are they?

DIANA (through headphones): The same people who want you to stop asking that question. Listen to me. You have about six minutes before the terminal's emergency lighting kicks back on and the security camera on the north wall reboots. When that happens, you need to be outside and on foot heading east on Washington. Don't use your car. Leave it.

CLAIRE: Who are you?

[PAUSE:1]

DIANA (through headphones): My name is Diana Voss. Ninety days ago, I became the fourth woman to disappear from Greenville, South Carolina. The difference between me and the other three is that I planned mine.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I have a rule — one I've kept for twelve years: when a source says something that changes the geometry of a story, you don't react. You record and you listen and you save the reaction for later, in private, where it belongs. I held to the rule. I don't know how.

[SFX: Headphone cable — slight movement, Claire adjusting her grip]

CLAIRE: Are you safe right now?

DIANA (through headphones): Safer than you are at the moment. Walk toward the south exit while I talk. Keep the headphones on — there's enough cable range in the locker relay.

[SFX: Footsteps — slow, deliberate, on old concrete — slightly hollow]

DIANA (through headphones): I'm a forensic accountant. I worked on contract for the Greenville municipal planning office and three private development firms over the past four years. That's how I knew Renata Osei — she was the senior city planner on the Reedy River corridor project. We worked together for eight months on the development overlay for the basin. We became close.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through headphones): Renata found something. An irregularity in the land-use approval records — pre-approval access granted to a private development consortium at sites along the river that hadn't been formally rezoned yet. Someone was treating the rezoning vote as already passed, months before it went to council.

CLAIRE: Who was giving that access?

DIANA (through headphones): Councilman Dale Merritt. He sits on the land use and development committee. He controls which rezoning applications reach the full council floor, and at what pace. And his name is on five separate LLC filings connected to the consortium that stands to control the Reedy River corridor if the vote passes.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through headphones): That vote is in ten days, Claire. A billion dollars in development rights. Flood-control land. Park-adjacent parcels. The entire river basin from downtown to the county line.

[SFX: Footsteps continuing — south end of terminal — faint light from a window above, just barely]

CLAIRE: And Renata found the irregularity. What happened to her?

DIANA (through headphones): She was approached first. A private security firm — Vantage Group, out of Charlotte — made contact three weeks after she flagged the records internally. They didn't threaten her directly. Not at first. They explained what would happen to her family's finances if she filed a formal complaint. Her husband's business contracts. Her daughter's graduate school funding. Renata was not a cowardly woman. She didn't capitulate. She started building a case file instead — quietly. That's the map in the suitcase.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through headphones): When Vantage realized she wasn't stopping, they took her somewhere quiet. A converted property on the river. Legally permitted — as a private event facility — under a variance attached to Merritt's rezoning package. His own language. His own approval. She has been there for eleven weeks.

CLAIRE: And the other two? Mara Fields and Mina Cho?

DIANA (through headphones): Mara discovered unauthorized land-clearing activity inside Falls Park — survey stakes and soil prep on protected land adjacent to the park boundary. She documented it. Trail log entries, GPS markers. She was going to file a formal report with the state park authority. Vantage intercepted her before she could. Mara has been held at the same property for nine weeks.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through headphones): Mina Cho. She was photographing the river corridor for a magazine piece — natural landscape documentation. She caught the land-clearing activity on camera. She also photographed a meeting between two Vantage representatives and a senior municipal official at the construction site. She didn't know what she had at the time. She brought the memory card to Renata. And then she disappeared.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I had covered all three disappearances as separate stories. Because there was nothing in the official record connecting them. And the official record was exactly what Vantage Group and Dale Merritt needed it to be.

[SFX: South terminal exit — faint light from a door window ahead]

CLAIRE: Where are they being held?

DIANA (through headphones): I'll get to that. First: how I know this. I was already suspicious of the rezoning financials before Renata disappeared — I'd flagged the same irregularity from a different access point, through one of the private development firms I contracted with. When Renata went missing, I understood immediately what it meant. I had a choice: report what I knew and become the fourth woman to disappear without anyone knowing why, or disappear on my own terms, keep moving, and find a way to get this to someone who could use it.

CLAIRE: So you staged your own disappearance.

DIANA (through headphones): I didn't stage anything. I simply didn't tell anyone where I was going. I've been mobile. I have resources. I've been watching the case, watching the suitcase placement, watching who responds. I needed to know which reporter would actually follow the chain.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through headphones): You photographed the contact number on a sealed evidence bag without the detective seeing you do it. You sat in your car for twenty minutes before you called. You drove to the terminal alone and put the headphones on. That's who I needed.

[PAUSE:1]

CLAIRE: And if I'd sent a tip to the detective instead of coming here myself?

DIANA (through headphones): Then the detective's lieutenant would have heard about it within an hour. And you'd have found yourself covering a story about how a contact number from a sealed evidence bag led nowhere.

CLAIRE: The lieutenant is compromised?

DIANA (through headphones): Three names in the municipal and law enforcement structure are on Vantage's contact list. I have documentation. But I won't tell you which names until I know the women are safe, because if you report the wrong name at the wrong moment, Merritt will know exactly where the information came from and those women will be moved.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: I was at the south exit. I could see the door. Six minutes was almost up. I had a hundred more questions, and one very immediate problem.

[SFX: Distant HVAC system — a deep hum, building slowly — emergency power cycling up]

DIANA (through headphones): Emergency lighting is about to come back. You need to go.

CLAIRE: How do I reach you?

DIANA (through headphones): You don't reach me. I reach you. Leave the headphones on the floor by the south exit. Walk out. Turn east on Washington Street.

CLAIRE: And then?

DIANA (through headphones): Then you go home, you write nothing down on any device connected to a network, and you wait. Tomorrow morning, your regular coffee stop on North Main — I'll have left something for you under the counter. A folder. Hard copies only. It has everything I know about the rezoning structure and the holding property.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through headphones): Claire. There's one more thing you need to understand before the lights come back.

CLAIRE: Tell me.

DIANA (through headphones): The three women are alive. They are being held, but they are not being harmed — Merritt doesn't want that level of federal attention. They are being told, every single day, what will happen to their families if they speak. And they believe it, because Vantage has demonstrated it in small ways. Job losses. A car accident. A child's scholarship rescinded.

[BEAT]

DIANA (through headphones): They are not being held against their will in any way a camera crew could capture. If you go to the police right now, those women will be released within the hour, their families will face consequences within the week, and the vote will pass unopposed. We need the vote stopped first. We need it stopped with evidence in hand.

[PAUSE:1]

DIANA (through headphones): The field notes. Mara's field notes from the park. There's a location marker on the last entry — before it cuts off. She hid a second copy.

CLAIRE: Where?

[SFX: Overhead lighting — first section — a fluorescent flicker beginning at the far end]

DIANA (through headphones): Falls Park. The overlook trail, three-tenths of a mile past the suspension bridge, there's a marked survey stake on the east side of the trail. Mara put a waterproof capsule two feet off the trail at the base of a birch cluster — three trees, white bark, the middle one has a rusted old blaze mark at eye level. Find it.

[SFX: Emergency lighting — spreading, section by section, clicking on]

DIANA (through headphones): Go. Now.

[SFX: Footsteps — quick now — toward the south exit door]

NARRATOR: I left the headphones on the floor by the door. I pushed out into the morning air on West Washington. I turned east. I walked five blocks to a coffee shop, ordered something I don't remember drinking, and sat in a corner booth until my hands stopped shaking.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: Three women alive, held in a building permitted under a rezoning vote ten days from now. A forensic accountant living off-grid with documentation of the whole architecture of it. A folder waiting for me tomorrow morning at my regular coffee stop.

[BEAT]

NARRATOR: And a second copy of Mara Fields' field notes, hidden three-tenths of a mile past a suspension bridge in Falls Park.

[PAUSE:2]

NARRATOR: That was the moment I understood that whoever Diana Voss was — however long she'd been watching, however carefully she'd planned this — she needed me to be good at my job. Which meant I had about eighteen hours to decide if I was.

MARA: In the center of the room, on a stone platform that was part of the floor's original construction: the bell.

MARA: It was not large. That surprised me. I had been hearing it in my imagination as something monumental — the Camperdown mill bell, the bell that warned of rising water, the bell that gave the whole infrastructure its operating logic. The actual bell was the size of a church bell from a small congregation. Iron-dark, hung from a bracket that had been bolted to the platform with a precision that spoke of someone who intended it to stay.

MARA: Someone had placed it here. Not here as part of the mill's engineering. Here deliberately, after everything else — placed at the end of the route, in a chamber that would be reached only by someone who had followed every checkpoint.

VICTOR DALLEN: The mill bell was decommissioned when the Camperdown flooded in 1974. The flood-watch system ran by token after that — no bell, just the checkpoint route. This bell is not the original.

MARA: Someone brought it here.

VICTOR DALLEN: Someone brought it here after the system was decommissioned and before 1997. It was here when Thomas and Luis and I reached the end of our route.

[BEAT]

LENA: Where are the bodies?

VICTOR DALLEN: The far wall. There's a sealed alcove behind the stone partition. Thomas and Luis were placed there — I know because I came back, years later, when I was certain enough of the space to re-enter it. I confirmed they were there. VICTOR DALLEN: I came back for them. More than once. It was the least I could do and also the most, and I have known the difference between those two things for twenty-nine years.

MARA: Against the wall to our left: a wooden cabinet with a combination lock that had been set to a combination and never spun. Open. Inside: the ledger.

MARA: Not a copy. The original Camperdown night-watch ledger — the unofficial one, the one Eli had found referenced in documents but never located physically. One hundred and forty pages of flood-watch records, checkpoint reports, the upright and inverted token entries, and, on the last completed page, the three names of the workers who had signed into checkpoint three at 11:42 PM on June 18, 1997, and — here, in the physical original — a fourth line, added later, in different ink.

MARA: Arthur Brenn. Checkpoint five. 11:38 PM.

MARA: He had arrived before them. He had been waiting.

ELI: He found the ledger before your mother did. He couldn't destroy it — too many people had seen references to it, would have noticed its absence. So he had it moved. To the one place where his own authority over the infrastructure gave him access and no one else's did.

ELI: The Basin Restoration project. Access authority to the subsurface corridor. All he needed was to get into this room and remove the ledger before anyone else reached it.

ELI: She documented everything she saw. Then filed the affidavit. Then disappeared.

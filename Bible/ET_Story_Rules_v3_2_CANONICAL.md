## EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Version 3.0 reorients Endless Tales storytelling around a single governing question:

> **Would a listener immediately start the next episode?**

Everything else is downstream of this question.

Version 2.0 was a technically excellent production document. It defined format, voice, author profiles, and ASC requirements with precision. What it did not define was *why* those rules exist — and what the stories must accomplish for the listener.

Version 3.0 establishes retention as the primary mission of every story. It adds the NEDS scoring framework to make retention measurable, makes series the primary vehicle for listener loyalty, creates a Franchise Candidate evaluation system for enduring characters, and upgrades the cliffhanger standard from a rule into a scoring criterion.

All technical rules from v2.0 are preserved unless explicitly deprecated.

## PART I — PRIMARY MISSION

### The Mission

The purpose of an Endless Tales story is to create listener desire for the next episode.

Not to educate. Not to inspire. Not to deliver a message.

To make the listener want more.

Education, inspiration, and meaning can be present — and often make stories better. But they are outputs, not inputs. They emerge from well-crafted stories. They are never the starting point.

### The Primary Test

At the end of every non-finale episode, ask:

**Would a listener immediately start the next episode?**

If the answer is no — or even "probably" — the story has failed its primary objective. Not its secondary objectives. Its primary one.

### The Meal Rule

> The story is the meal.
> The lesson is seasoning.

A meal with no seasoning is bland. A meal that is entirely seasoning is inedible.

Stories with no theme are forgettable. Stories that exist to deliver a theme are lectures. Endless Tales produces neither.

## ENTERTAINMENT FIRST RULE

The primary purpose of an Endless Tales story is to **entertain**.

Listeners come for:
- Suspense
- Curiosity
- Emotion
- Mystery
- Wonder
- Humor
- Fear
- Connection
- The desire to know what happens next

**Never interrupt a story to teach a lesson.**

**Never have the narrator explain the meaning of the story.**

**Never include speeches whose primary purpose is to educate, persuade, moralize, lecture, or preach.**

**Never allow a character to become the author's mouthpiece.**

### Meaning Must Be Earned

If a story contains a lesson, insight, emotional truth, philosophical idea, or moral theme, it must emerge **naturally** through:

- Character choices
- Consequences
- Conflict
- Sacrifice
- Failure
- Success
- Events

**The listener should discover meaning. The writer should not explain meaning.**

### Questions Over Answers

Questions are usually more powerful than answers.

### What Stories Must Never Feel Like

- Sermons
- Lectures
- Essays
- Political arguments
- Social commentary pieces
- Self-help content disguised as fiction

### The Priority

**Story first.**

**Theme second.**

**Lesson last.**

> The story is the meal.
> The lesson is seasoning.

## PART II — THE ENDLESS TALES LISTENER PROMISE

### What We Promise

Every Endless Tales story should deliver at least one of the following:

- **A mystery worth solving** — An unanswered question that drives the listener forward. Not all mysteries; just one that matters.
- **A capable person facing a difficult problem** — Someone good at what they do, tested beyond their current capability. The gap between skill and challenge is where the listener leans in.
- **A meaningful relationship under pressure** — Two or more characters whose connection must survive conflict. The relationship is the story.
- **A hidden truth waiting to be uncovered** — Something concealed. Something that, when revealed, changes how the listener understands everything that came before.
- **A world the listener wants to spend more time in** — A setting so compelling, so fully realized, that the listener cares as much about remaining in that world as about resolving the plot.

### What We Avoid

Every Endless Tales story must avoid:

- **Boredom** — Empty stretches with no forward momentum. Distracted listeners will not return.
- **Confusion** — Stories that require the listener to keep extensive notes or replay scenes to follow. Commuters cannot do this.
- **Lectures** — Characters explaining themes, stating lessons, or delivering wisdom speeches. The listener is not a student.
- **Pointless complexity** — Layers of plot that don't serve the story. Complexity is permitted; pointless complexity is not.
- **Meaningless endings** — Resolutions that feel arbitrary or unearned. Every ending must deliver something — catharsis, revelation, or transformation.

### The Core Commitment

**The listener should always feel their time was respected.**

A 30-minute story is a 30-minute commitment. The listener gave it to you. This promise is how you earn the next 30 minutes.

## PART III — COMMUTER REALITY RULE

### The Audience

The primary audience is:

- Commuters in cars
- Drivers on long routes
- Workers with ambient listening time
- Exercisers with divided attention
- Distracted listeners (everywhere)

The listener is not sitting in a dark theater with their phone off. They are behind a wheel or on a treadmill or multitasking at a workstation. They may briefly lose focus. They may miss 30 seconds of dialogue. They may reset their attention at any moment.

### The Standard

Stories must remain understandable if the listener misses 30 seconds of audio.

This is not a suggestion. It is a production requirement.

### Requirements for Commuter Compliance

**1. Regular Re-orientation**

After every scene break, the narrator or dialogue must establish:
- Who we are with
- Where we are
- What time it is (if relevant)
- What just happened (in one sentence)

Do not assume continuity from the last scene. A listener may have been interrupted.

**2. Clear Character Identification**

Character names must be stated at least once after every scene break or significant time jump. Do not rely on character voice alone. Use the narrator or dialogue to name them.

> ✅ `NARRATOR: Deputy Hale returned to the canyon just after dawn.`
> ❌ `NARRATOR: He returned to the canyon.` *(If the listener missed the previous scene setup, they don't know who "he" is)*

**3. Clear Stakes**

What is at risk in this scene? The listener must know within 30 seconds. If the listener tunes back in mid-scene, they should understand what matters.

**4. Clear Scene Transitions**

Signal when we are moving to a new place or time.

> ✅ `[SFX: car engine, doors open]` `NARRATOR: Three miles south, at the sheriff's office...`
> ❌ Silence, then a new scene with no transition signal. The listener is confused.

### The Re-Orientation Checklist

Before finalizing any script, verify:

- [ ] Every scene break includes character re-identification
- [ ] Stakes are stated or demonstrated in the first 30 seconds of any scene
- [ ] Time/place transitions are marked with SFX or narrator cues
- [ ] No critical plot information appears in a single throwaway line
- [ ] A listener who missed one exchange could still follow the story

This is Vega's gate for audio approval. Scripts that fail the re-orientation checklist do not advance to Ready For Review.

## PART IV — CORE PHILOSOPHY

### The Seven Retention Pillars

Every Endless Tales story is evaluated against seven retention properties. Not all stories hit all seven. A 5–7 episode series should hit all seven before the finale. A standalone must hit at least four.

**1. Curiosity**
The listener must have an active, unanswered question at every moment. Not a vague sense of interest — a specific open loop. *Who left those tracks? What is she hiding? Why did he go back?*

**2. Competence**
Protagonists who are good at things are more compelling than protagonists who are helpless. The listener enjoys watching a capable person face a problem that exceeds their capability. The gap between what the protagonist can do and what the situation requires is where tension lives.

**3. Escalation**
Each episode must end in a state of higher stakes than it began. Not just different stakes — higher. The world must be more complicated, more dangerous, or more revealing at the end than at the start.

**4. Emotional Investment**
The listener must care what happens to at least one character. This is non-negotiable. Plot tension without character investment is a puzzle. Stories are not puzzles.

**5. Cliffhangers**
Non-finale episodes must end with an unresolved hook of sufficient force that the next episode becomes genuinely difficult to resist. See the Cliffhanger Rule (Part IV) and the NEDS framework (Part V).

**6. Consistent Quality**
One weak episode in a series damages listener trust in all subsequent episodes. The quality floor matters as much as the quality ceiling. Listeners forgive a single miss; they do not return after two.

**7. Listener Belonging**
Over time, the best Endless Tales series should make a listener feel they belong to a world — that they know these characters, understand this place, and would miss it if it ended. This is the franchise condition. It cannot be manufactured; it must be earned through quality across multiple episodes.

## PART V — SERIES-FIRST FRAMEWORK

### The Series-First Rule

Endless Tales is a series platform first.

Standalones serve four purposes:
- Discovery (a first-listen by a new subscriber)
- Onboarding (introducing a new listener to a genre or author)
- Testing (validating a new author voice, genre, or setting before series commitment)
- Variety (palette cleansers between longer series for committed listeners)

Standalones are not the primary retention vehicle. Series are.

A listener who finishes a standalone has consumed a complete experience. A listener who finishes a series episode has an incomplete experience by design. Incompleteness is the engine of return.

### Preferred Series Lengths

| Length | Use Case |
|--------|----------|
| 3 episodes | Tight genre premise, high concept, minimal character setup required |
| 5 episodes | Standard series unit — most protagonists and worlds fit here |
| 7 episodes | Complex multi-thread stories, deeper world-building, ensemble casts |
| 13 episodes | Full season format — major franchise-candidate series only |

Series shorter than 3 episodes provide insufficient runway for emotional investment. Series longer than 13 episodes without a clear arc plan risk losing narrative coherence. Both require explicit Marc approval.

### Series Architecture Requirements

Every series must be designed before Episode 1 is written. Required before production:

1. **The Central Question** — What question does this series answer? (One sentence.)
2. **The Protagonist Arc** — How does the protagonist change from Episode 1 to finale?
3. **The Escalation Map** — A brief description of how stakes increase from episode to episode.
4. **The Finale Resolution** — What does the ending deliver? (Not the content — the emotional type: justice, sacrifice, transformation, revelation, etc.)
5. **The Cliffhanger Chain** — For each non-finale episode, one sentence describing the specific hook.

The Cliffhanger Chain must be written before Episode 1 enters production. A series without a defined cliffhanger chain is not ready to produce.

## PART VI — CLIFFHANGER RULE

### The Standard

Non-finale episodes must end with one of the following:

- **Revelation:** New information that reframes everything the listener just heard
- **Danger:** A character placed in immediate threat with no resolution
- **Betrayal:** An alliance broken, a trust violated, an assumption destroyed
- **Discovery:** Something found, uncovered, or revealed that opens a new and larger problem
- **Decision:** A character forced to an irreversible choice the listener cannot predict

### What Constitutes a Weak Cliffhanger

The following are not acceptable cliffhangers:

| Weak Pattern | Why It Fails |
|---|---|
| "Things are going to get worse" | Vague. No specific hook. |
| Character escapes one danger only to face another of the same type | Repetitive peril. Listener trust erodes. |
| Cliffhanger resolved in the first 60 seconds of the next episode | Signals the writer doesn't believe in it. |
| A new character appears and says something ominous | Misdirection without substance. |
| "Little did she know..." | Telegraphed dread. Not a cliffhanger. |
| Weather, traffic, or mechanical failure as a substitute for stakes | Circumstantial peril. Not a story cliffhanger. |

### Fake Cliffhanger Detection

A fake cliffhanger feels urgent but contains no actual information. A real cliffhanger contains specific, unanswered information that the listener cannot resolve without the next episode.

Test: Can the cliffhanger be written as a specific question?

> ✅ *"Who was the second person in the canyon that night?"* — Real. Specific. Unanswerable without Episode 4.
> ❌ *"What will happen to Deputy Hale?"* — Fake. Too broad. Applies to every episode.
> ✅ *"Did Cross arrange the sheriff's death — and does Lucy know?"* — Real. Two specific questions raised simultaneously.
> ❌ *"The danger was only beginning."* — Fake. Vague escalation signal, not a cliffhanger.

## PART VII — NEDS FRAMEWORK
### Next Episode Desire Score

Every non-finale episode receives a NEDS score before production is finalized. The score is evaluated at the brief stage, not after production.

A score below 7 requires revision before production begins. A score below 7 after production requires repair before release.

### Scoring Rubric

| Category | Scoring | Description |
|----------|---------|-------------|
| **Curiosity** | 0–3 | 3: Multiple active open loops; 2: One strong open loop; 1: Weak or single minor question; 0: No unresolved questions |
| **Stakes** | 0–2 | 2: Stakes are personal, consequential, and specific; 1: Stakes are present but generic; 0: No clear stakes |
| **Escalation** | 0–2 | 2: Episode ends in demonstrably higher stakes than it began; 1: Modest escalation; 0: Same stakes or lower at end |
| **Emotional Investment** | 0–2 | 2: Strong attachment to at least one character; 1: Mild connection; 0: No reason to care about outcomes |
| **Cliffhanger** | 0–1 | 1: Episode ends on a specific, compelling, unresolvable hook; 0: No cliffhanger, weak cliffhanger, or fake cliffhanger |

**Maximum score: 10**

### Score Interpretations

| Score | Rating | Action |
|-------|--------|--------|
| 9–10 | Excellent | Proceed to production |
| 8 | Strong | Proceed to production |
| 7 | Acceptable | Review weakest category before proceeding |
| 5–6 | Weak | Revise before production. Do not proceed. |
| 0–4 | Failing | Full brief revision required |

### NEDS for Standalones

Standalones do not receive a Cliffhanger score (0/1 not applicable). Maximum score is 9.

Standalone target: 7+. A standalone below 6 will not generate return visits and should be revised or not produced.

### NEDS Evaluation — Who Scores

| Role | Responsibility |
|------|---------------|
| Hal | Provides initial NEDS score with each story brief submission |
| Orion | Reviews any brief scoring 7 or below — escalates to Marc if 6 or below |
| Marc | Final authority on NEDS disputes and any production approval below 7 |
| Vega | Reviews NEDS score against finished audio — flags emotional investment failures |

## PART VIII — COMPETENCE RULE

### The Principle

Listeners enjoy watching intelligent people solve difficult problems.

Capable protagonists create audience identification through admiration, not pity. The listener leans in because they want to see how this specific person uses their specific skills to address this specific problem.

### Preferred Protagonist Types

The following profession types are strongly preferred as Endless Tales protagonists:

- Detectives and investigators
- Dispatchers and communications operators
- Engineers and technical specialists
- Pilots, captains, and vehicle operators
- Entrepreneurs under pressure
- Journalists and researchers
- Nurses, paramedics, and emergency responders
- Lawyers, prosecutors, and defense attorneys
- Rescuers and search-and-rescue specialists
- Scientists and analysts

### The Competence-Stakes Ratio

The stronger the protagonist's competence, the higher the stakes must be to generate tension.

A highly competent protagonist facing a minor problem produces no tension.
A highly competent protagonist facing a problem that exceeds their competence produces maximum tension.

> The detective who solves every case must face a case that cannot be solved.
> The dispatcher who saves every caller must face a call she cannot save.

Design the problem to exceed the protagonist's capability. Not crush it — exceed it.

### Helpless Protagonists

Protagonists who exist primarily to react, suffer, or be rescued are not preferred.

If a protagonist cannot demonstrate competence in at least one domain, reconsider the characterization. Suffering is not a character trait. Helplessness is not relatable — it is exhausting.

## PART IX — CHARACTER RETENTION & CHARACTER ATTACHMENT RULE

### The Principle

Listeners often return for characters more than plots.

A great plot can be replaced by the next plot. A great character cannot be replaced.

The long-term value of an Endless Tales series is measured not by individual episode quality but by whether the protagonist has become someone listeners want to spend time with across multiple stories.

### The Character Attachment Question

After every series finale, ask:

> **Would a listener want another story with this protagonist even if the current mystery is completely solved?**

The strongest retention franchises are character-driven, not plot-driven. Examples: Bosch, Reacher, Sherlock Holmes, Poirot, Jack Ryan. The mystery may end. The protagonist continues.

### Character Attachment & Franchise Candidate Evaluation

Upon completion of a series finale, Hal submits a Character Attachment Evaluation to Orion within 5 business days. This evaluation determines both character retention potential and franchise candidate status.

**Evaluation Framework:**

| Criterion | 0 pts | 1 pt | 2 pts |
|-----------|-------|------|-------|
| **Character Appeal** | No audience attachment observed; character is a vehicle for plot only | Mild listener attachment; character has some memorable traits | Strong audience connection; character has distinct personality and unresolved interior life |
| **World Depth** | World exists only for this story; minimal secondary characters or locations | Partial world—some secondary characters or unexplored locations possible | Rich world with multiple secondary characters, locations, and unresolved relationships |
| **Future Story Potential** | Series has no natural continuation; protagonist's arc is fully closed | Some potential for continuation; one or two story directions possible | Multiple natural directions for new stories emerge organically from the world established |
| **Listener Return Potential** | Series completion rate is below 70%; listener behavior shows minimal return interest | Series completion rate 70–85%; replay rate is low | Series completion rate 85%+; listener behavior indicates strong desire for more content with this character |

**Total Score: 0–8 points**

### Character Attachment Score Interpretation

| Score | Rating | Decision |
|-------|--------|----------|
| 7–8 | Strong | Recommend franchise continuation to Marc; character is a long-term asset |
| 5–6 | Moderate | Optional continuation — Marc decides based on catalog strategy; character has potential |
| 3–4 | Weak | Series complete; character may not sustain a sequel; consider protagonist retirement |
| 0–2 | Failing | Do not continue. Protagonist is complete. Move on to new characters. |

### Franchise Candidate vs. Series Complete

**Franchise Candidate (Score 5+):**
- Additional seasons are encouraged and expected
- Character continuity is the retention engine
- Prototype for additional seasons must be approved by Marc before production

**Series Complete (Score 0–4):**
- The story is finished
- The character does not extend to new narratives
- If another story with this protagonist exists, it is a separate creative decision, not a continuation

### Continuation Rules for Franchise Characters

If a character scores 5+ on Character Attachment, additional seasons of their franchise must:

1. **Raise the stakes** beyond the previous season — not repeat the same threat or conflict type
2. **Develop the protagonist** in a new direction — show new facets, new growth, new challenges; do not simply repeat their arc
3. **Introduce significant new elements** — at least one new major character, threat, location, or world development
4. **Receive full series design approval** from Marc before Episode 1 enters production

## PART X — HIDDEN LESSON RULE

### The Principle

Stories can carry meaning. They should not carry messages.

A message is delivered. A meaning is discovered.

When a listener discovers something through a character's choices, mistakes, and consequences, they own that insight. When a character explains the lesson, the listener is a passive recipient. Passive recipients do not return.

### The Standard

Hidden lessons are optional. They must never dominate the story.

Characters must never:
- Explain the theme of the story
- Summarize what the experience meant
- Deliver wisdom speeches at the end of episodes
- Act as philosophical mouthpieces for the author

Characters must:
- Make decisions that reveal character
- Face consequences that carry meaning
- Change in ways the listener recognizes as true

### The Discovery Test

If the lesson can be extracted as a single sentence and stated without reference to any plot event, the story has probably made the lesson too explicit.

> ❌ *"Sometimes the thing you're looking for was inside you all along."* — Explicit. Extracted from plot. Lecture.
> ✅ A character who spends three episodes looking for external answers and finds the solution through a moment of personal honesty — the listener feels this. It is never stated.

### What This Replaces

Version 2.0 did not explicitly address lesson delivery. Many early Endless Tales scripts ended with protagonist monologues summarizing the moral of the story. This pattern is deprecated in v3.0.

**Deprecated pattern:**
> Final narrator line that begins "She had learned that day that..." or "He understood now that..." or "The truth was that..."

**v3.0 standard:**
> Final narrator line that creates a specific, concrete image, action, or observation. The listener draws their own conclusion.

## PART XI — BELIEVABILITY RULE

### The Standard

Default to believable.

Unless the genre specifically requires otherwise (horror, supernatural, sci-fi), stories should take place in a world that feels real, obeys real-world logic, and involves characters whose behavior the listener recognizes as human.

Weird for its own sake erodes trust. Implausibility breaks immersion. A distracted driver who catches an implausible detail will mentally leave the story and may not return.

### Genre Exceptions

| Genre | Believability Standard |
|-------|----------------------|
| Thriller, Mystery, Crime, Drama, Western, Adventure | Strict — real-world logic throughout |
| Horror | Believable setup; supernatural elements introduced gradually |
| Supernatural | Internally consistent rules required — establish them early |
| Sci-Fi | Near-future or believable-science preferred; hard science fiction permitted |
| Historical Drama | Period-accurate; no anachronistic behavior or values |

### The Commitment Rule

If a story commits to a genre that permits the unbelievable, it must be internally consistent.

Horror stories can have ghosts. They cannot have ghosts that follow no discernible rules.
Sci-fi stories can have technology that doesn't exist. That technology must behave consistently.

Inconsistency within a genre is more damaging than genre commitment. A fully committed ghost story is more believable than a ghost story with inconsistent ghost rules.

## PART XII — FORMAT RULES (TECHNICAL STANDARD)

*All rules from v2.0 are carried forward unchanged unless explicitly noted.*

### Script Header (Required)

Every script must include a complete header block before the script begins:

```
SERIES:
SERIES_TOTAL_EPISODES:
SERIES_IS_FINALE:
EPISODE:
EPISODE_TITLE:
AUTHOR:
GENRE:
DESCRIPTION:
NARRATOR:
ANNOUNCER:
NARRATIVE_VOICE:
NARRATOR_IS_CHARACTER:
SUNO PROMPT:
NEDS_SCORE: [total]/10  ← NEW in v3.0
```

`NEDS_SCORE` is required on all scripts from v3.0 forward. Hal provides this score with each brief.

### Dialogue Format (r5)

`CHARACTER NAME: dialogue text` — all caps name, colon, then text.

No parentheticals inside dialogue lines. ASC strips them. They will not be heard.

Convey tone through word choice and NARRATOR lines, not parenthetical directions.

### Dialogue Separation Rule
*Approved by Marc Postlewaite, 2026-06-10*

A character may not speak two consecutive lines without an intervening element. Between any two lines from the same character there must be one of: a NARRATOR line, a [BEAT], a [PAUSE], a physical action description, or a line from another character.

*Rationale:* In audio, consecutive lines from the same voice blend together and sound like a stutter or repetition. Even a two-word narrator bridge ("He waited." / "She looked away.") creates the audio space the second line needs to land as a new beat.

*Example (failing):*
```
VICTOR: You work for Hendricks Financial. With a sister in Portland.
VICTOR: Your routine is very predictable.
```
*Example (passing):*
```
VICTOR: You work for Hendricks Financial. With a sister in Portland.
NARRATOR: He let that sit.
VICTOR: Your routine is very predictable.
```

### SFX Format (r4)

`[SFX: description]` on its own line, never inline.

### Pause Cues (r6)

`[BEAT]` for 1-second pause. `[PAUSE:X]` for longer pauses. Always on their own line.

### Character Guide (r2)

List each character: name, age, gender, accent, personality. Used for voice casting.

### Suno Prompt (r3)

Include in header — 2–3 sentences describing genre, instrumentation, tempo, mood.

### Description (r14)

24 words or fewer. Present tense. Designed to make a driver press play. No spoilers.

> ✅ *"A long-haul trucker's final delivery holds a secret that could cost him everything."*
> ❌ *"This is a story about a trucker who discovers something surprising in his cargo."*

## PART XIII — NARRATIVE VOICE RULES

*Carried forward from v2.0 unchanged.*

### NARRATIVE_VOICE Field (r22)

Every script must declare its narrative voice. Valid values:

- `first_person` — NARRATOR IS the protagonist. "I", "me", "my". Maximum intimacy.
- `third_limited` — Narrator follows one character from outside. "He/she/they". Their thoughts accessible; others' are not.
- `third_omniscient` — Narrator knows everything. Can move between characters. Documentary authority.
- `second_person` — Listener IS the protagonist. "You", "your". Rare. Requires Marc approval.

### Voice Consistency (r27)

The declared voice must be maintained throughout the script without exception. Mixed voice is a quality gate failure.

### Narrator-as-Character (NARRATOR_IS_CHARACTER)

A story may declare that its narrator is a character in the narrative. Requirements:

1. Declared in header as `NARRATOR_IS_CHARACTER: true`
2. Approved by Marc before production
3. Cast with a non-platform voice — never Belle B or default platform narrator

```
NARRATOR_IS_CHARACTER: true
NARRATOR_CHARACTER_NAME: [character name]
NARRATOR_CHARACTER_VOICE: [ElevenLabs voice ID — not a platform voice]
```

## PART XV — STRUCTURAL RULES

### Open With Action (r8)

Open with action or conflict, not exposition.

The first 30 seconds must contain something that creates a question. A description of weather, a scene of a character waking up, or an explanation of backstory are not openings — they are delays.

### Audio-Only Standard (r9)

Stories must work without visuals. All setting and action must be conveyed through dialogue, narration, or SFX.

Test: If the script were read aloud in a featureless room, would the listener know exactly where they are and what is happening?

### Distracted Listener Standard (r12)

The audience is driving. Stories must make sense even if the listener is briefly distracted.

This means:
- Character names should be stated at least once after a scene break
- No critical information may be conveyed in a single throwaway line
- Important reveals should have at least one callback or confirmation

### SFX Frequency (r11)

Include at least one significant SFX cue every 60–90 seconds of script.

### Dialogue Turn Length (r10)

Dialogue exchanges should be short — 1–3 sentences per turn for most exchanges.

Characters who deliver paragraph-length speeches mid-conversation are not in a story; they are in an essay.

### Narrator Usage

The NARRATOR is the listener's guide and the story's most powerful tool.

Use the narrator generously to:
- Introduce characters the moment they appear
- Orient the listener after scene transitions
- Convey atmosphere and tone
- Re-anchor a distracted listener who may have missed a line

The narrator should speak in short, punchy sentences. Long paragraphs of narration are to be avoided — break them into shorter beats with SFX or dialogue between.

## PART XVI — ANNOUNCER RULES

*Carried forward from v2.0 with additions.*

### No Time of Day (r18)

Neither intro nor outro may reference any time of day under any circumstances.

Forbidden: "good morning," "good evening," "tonight," "this morning," "today's episode," "join us tomorrow."

Listeners play stories at any hour. Time references are wrong for most listeners and break immersion for all of them.

### Intro Format (r19)

1. Platform name: *"Endless Tales presents..."*
2. Series name (if series) or story title
3. Episode title (if series)
4. One-sentence hook. Present tense. No spoilers.
5. Hard stop. Begin story immediately.

> ✅ `ANNOUNCER: Endless Tales presents... Deadwater Canyon. Episode Three: The Missing Hour. One body. One deputy. And a killer who was never a stranger.`
> ❌ `ANNOUNCER: Welcome back to Endless Tales! We're so excited to bring you another episode today!`

### Belle B Intro Standard
*Approved by Marc Postlewaite, 2026-06-10*

Every Belle B intro line must be story-specific. Generic phrases that could apply to any story are not acceptable.

Required elements:
1. Reference to the protagonist (name or clear description)
2. The inciting event or central conflict stated concretely
3. A tension-forward hook — what is at stake, or what question the listener will follow

Prohibited phrases: "Settle in for a story," "built to carry you," "carry you cleanly," or any language that could be copy-pasted to a different story without changing a word.

*Example (failing):* "This is The Witness, an Endless Tales Original. Settle in for a story built to carry you cleanly into its first turn."

*Example (passing):* "In The Witness, a woman sees a man commit a murder in a parking garage — and then realizes he knows she was there."

### Series Outro (r21)

The series episode outro must:
1. Tease the next episode with a specific named character, threat, or event that WILL appear
2. End with a question or provocative statement

Generic teasers are forbidden. Name something real.

> ✅ `ANNOUNCER: Next time on Deadwater Canyon — Sheriff Cole's widow opens his safe. What she finds will end everything Deputy Hale thought he knew about this town.`
> ❌ `ANNOUNCER: Next time on Deadwater Canyon — things get even more complicated. Don't miss it!`

### Standalone Outro (r20)

Must NOT include:
- Time of day references
- "Tune in next time," "we'll see you soon," "thanks for listening"
- Cliffhangers (standalones must resolve)

> ✅ `ANNOUNCER: That was "The Grave He Dug Himself" — an Endless Tales original. Written by Dale Harmon.`

## PART XVII — ENDING RULES

### Standalone Endings (r31)

Every standalone must:
1. Resolve the central conflict completely — no dangling threads
2. Signal clearly to the listener that the story is over
3. Deliver an emotional payoff — satisfaction, catharsis, or a resonant final image

The listener should never wonder "is that it?"

> ✅ `NARRATOR: Hale drove back through the canyon as the sun came up. For the first time in three years, he didn't check his mirrors.`
> ❌ `NARRATOR: Hale left town. Things would be different now.` *(vague, no emotional landing)*

The final NARRATOR line of a standalone is the last image the listener carries. Make it land.

### Standalone Completion Standard
*Approved by Marc Postlewaite, 2026-06-10*

A standalone story is complete when two conditions are both met:

1. **External resolution:** The antagonist, threat, or central conflict reaches a definitive outcome — neutralized, transformed, or concluded.
2. **Protagonist acknowledgment:** The protagonist experiences a moment of recognition. Not necessarily triumph or comfort, but clarity — they know what happened and what it means to them.

A story where the external threat is resolved but the protagonist's interior experience is left entirely unexamined does not meet this standard.

**Ambiguous endings:** A story may end with unresolved fear or ongoing uncertainty — but only if the preceding scenes have built toward that ambiguity explicitly. Ambiguous endings that arrive without preparation read as incomplete, not literary.

### Series Episode Endings (r32)

All non-finale series episodes must end with a hard cliffhanger per Part IV.

See NEDS framework (Part V) — the Cliffhanger score is a mandatory evaluation criterion.

### Series Finale Endings

The series finale must:
1. Resolve ALL major story threads — every question raised must be answered or deliberately closed
2. Give the protagonist a clear and earned outcome — victory, defeat, transformation, or peace — not ambiguity
3. Signal clearly that the story is complete
4. Formally close the series in the ANNOUNCER outro

> ✅ `ANNOUNCER: That was the final episode of Deadwater Canyon — an Endless Tales original series by Marc Hobelman. Six episodes. One truth.`

Series finales must NOT:
- End on a cliffhanger
- Leave major character fates unresolved
- Use "perhaps," "someday," or "who knows" as substitutes for resolution
- Tease a sequel in the outro (save that for a separate announcement, handled through the Franchise Candidate process)

### Series Episode Count Declaration (r33)

Every series episode must declare total episode count and finale status in the header:

```
SERIES_TOTAL_EPISODES: 6
SERIES_IS_FINALE: false
```

When `SERIES_IS_FINALE: true`, r16 (cliffhanger requirement) does not apply. Finale ending rules apply.

## PART XVIII — PLATFORM VOICE PROTECTION

*Carried forward from v2.0 unchanged.*

### Platform Voices Are Not Characters (r29)

Belle B and the default platform narrator voice are reserved exclusively for their platform roles.

They may never be cast as characters inside a story script — no dispatcher characters, no radio host characters, no in-story roles of any kind.

### Platform Voice Exception

A narrator-as-character may be used only when:
1. `NARRATOR_IS_CHARACTER: true` is declared in the header
2. Marc has approved the casting before production
3. The voice assigned is not a platform voice

## PART XIX — CONTENT STANDARD

### Content Guidelines (r13)

Avoid:
- Graphic violence with gratuitous detail
- Explicit sexual content
- Material designed primarily to disturb or distress

Conflict, danger, death, betrayal, and moral complexity are all permitted and encouraged. These are the materials of drama.

The standard is: would this be appropriate for a late-night cable drama? If yes, it is within the Endless Tales content range.

### ASC Strip List

The following are silently removed from dialogue before voice generation. Do not rely on them:

- Parenthetical tone directions: `(quietly)`, `(to himself)`, `(sharply)`, `(low)`, `(calling out)`
- Inline `[BEAT]` markers inside dialogue lines
- Inline `[PAUSE:X]` markers inside dialogue lines
- Inline `[SFX: ...]` markers inside dialogue lines

Always place `[BEAT]`, `[PAUSE:X]`, and `[SFX: ...]` on dedicated lines.

## PART XX — IMPLEMENTATION RECOMMENDATIONS

### For Claude (Story Writing)

1. **Begin every brief evaluation with NEDS.** Before writing a word of script, score the brief against the NEDS framework. If the score is below 7, request a brief revision from Hal rather than producing a weak script.

2. **Write the cliffhanger first.** Know how the episode ends before writing how it begins. A cliffhanger written in retrospect is usually a fake cliffhanger. A cliffhanger written first shapes the entire episode.

3. **Apply the Competence Rule at the protagonist level.** Before writing scene one, ask: what is this protagonist specifically good at? How does this episode force them to use that skill under conditions that exceed their capability?

4. **Apply the Lesson Test at the final narrator line.** If the final line states a lesson explicitly, revise it. The final line should be a specific image, action, or moment — not a conclusion.

5. **Score your own output.** With each completed episode brief, submit a self-NEDS score with brief justification per category. This is required from v3.0 forward.

6. **Carry consequence forward.** At least one major development from each episode (a death, discovery, alliance shift, or power change) must be explicitly reflected in the next episode. Characters are not reset between episodes.

### For Hal (Production Oversight)

1. **NEDS is a production gate.** Any brief scoring below 7 does not enter production. Hal returns it to Claude with specific improvement notes per NEDS category.

2. **Series design precedes Episode 1 production.** The Cliffhanger Chain for all non-finale episodes must be reviewed by Hal before Episode 1 is dispatched to ASC. A series without a defined cliffhanger chain is not ready.

3. **Flag competence gaps.** If a protagonist has no demonstrated domain of competence in the first episode, flag this before Episode 2 enters production. It is easier to fix in the brief than in the audio.

4. **Track NEDS scores across a series.** If NEDS scores decline across episodes (e.g., Episode 1 scored 9, Episode 3 scored 7), escalate to Orion before Episode 4 enters production. A declining NEDS arc is a series quality warning.

5. **Character Attachment evaluation is mandatory.** Submit a Character Attachment Evaluation to Orion within 5 business days of every series finale entering the catalog. Use the Character Attachment Scoring framework (Part IX). This evaluation determines both character retention and franchise candidate status.

6. **Commuter Reality Checklist is a brief gate.** Before final approval of any brief, Hal verifies the re-orientation checklist (Part III):
   - [ ] Every scene break includes character re-identification
   - [ ] Stakes are established in the first 30 seconds of each scene
   - [ ] Time/place transitions are marked with SFX or narrator cues
   - [ ] No critical information appears in a single throwaway line
   - [ ] A listener who missed one exchange could still follow the story

Briefs that fail the re-orientation checklist do not advance to production.

### For Vega (Quality Review)

1. **NEDS is an audio standard, not just a brief standard.** Vega evaluates finished audio against the Emotional Investment category (0–2) specifically. A story that scored 8 on the brief may still fail emotional investment in production if voice performances are flat.

2. **Cliffhanger audio delivery is a Vega checkpoint.** The final 30 seconds of every non-finale episode must be reviewed by Vega specifically for cliffhanger impact. A great cliffhanger written poorly is a failed cliffhanger.

3. **Commuter Reality Audit is mandatory before Ready For Review.** Before any audio advances to Ready For Review, Vega verifies the re-orientation checklist against the finished audio:
   - Character names stated after each scene break
   - Stakes established within 30 seconds of any scene start
   - Scene transitions clearly marked with SFX and/or narrator orientation
   - No critical plot information in a single throwaway line
   - A listener who missed one exchange could still follow the story
   
   Audio that fails the re-orientation audit is returned to Hal for revision before approval.

4. **Apply the listener test.** At least one person unfamiliar with the script should hear each episode before it advances to Ready For Review. Vega coordinates this review. The question: *"Did you want to hear the next one?"* This provides listener demand feedback for Character Attachment evaluation.

5. **Flag Lesson Violations.** If a final narrator line explicitly states a theme, lesson, or moral, flag it for revision before approval. This is a content quality standard in v3.0, not a technical issue.

### For Orion (Operations)

1. **NEDS gate enforcement.** Orion confirms that no story brief with a score below 7 advances to production without Marc's explicit written approval. This is an operational gate, not a recommendation.

2. **Character Attachment pipeline.** Orion receives Character Attachment Evaluations from Hal within 5 business days of every series finale. Orion reviews each evaluation and:
   - Score 7–8: Recommend franchise continuation to Marc; mark character as long-term asset
   - Score 5–6: Submit to Marc with notation that continuation is optional based on catalog strategy
   - Score 3–4: Recommend protagonist retirement; series is complete
   - Score 0–2: Do not continue; character does not sustain sequels

3. **Series design audit.** Before any new series enters production, Orion confirms: (a) series length is an approved format, (b) Central Question is defined, (c) Cliffhanger Chain is documented, (d) Finale Resolution type is defined, (e) Commuter Reality compliance checklist is reviewed. Missing items block production.

4. **Declining NEDS escalation.** Any series where three consecutive episode NEDS scores fall below 7 is escalated to Marc as a catalog risk item, regardless of whether individual episodes were approved for production.

5. **Commuter Reality enforcement.** Orion tracks re-orientation audit results from Vega. Any story that fails the Commuter Reality checklist is flagged before advancing to catalog. This is a content gate, not a formatting preference.

6. **Weekly NEDS + Character Attachment reporting.** Orion includes aggregate NEDS scores and pending Character Attachment evaluations in the weekly readiness review. Target: catalog average NEDS of 8+ across all content in Ready For Review. Target: franchise pipeline maintains 2–3 character continuations in active development.

## PART XXII — PRODUCTION / AUDIO STANDARD

### Outro Music Behavior — Required for All Episodes
*Approved by Marc Postlewaite, 2026-06-10*

The final mix must implement three-phase outro music behavior:
1. **Swell:** As the last line of narrative text ends, music volume rises slightly (~+3dB over 2 seconds)
2. **Duck:** When Belle B begins her outro line, music ducks under Belle's voice to approximately 25% of narrative level
3. **Fade:** After Belle B finishes, music fades to complete silence over exactly 3 seconds

This applies to all standalone and series episodes. The render_final_mix pipeline step implements this standard automatically. Writers do not need to notate it in the script.
